# gridfinity-market-check-20261017

Task: check whether a paid Gridfinity storage-bin file pack can sell, before a small team builds one — paid listings across Printables, MakerWorld, Cults3D, Thangs, Etsy and Gumroad; the free alternatives; buyer-request gaps quoted from comments; and platform fees from official pages.

Read date for all pages: 2026-10-17. Public pages only; no login, no purchase, no messages sent.

## Bottom line

Gridfinity money is not where the task assumed it is. The system itself is open — Zack Freedman released Gridfinity under the **MIT license** (per the community wiki gridfinity.xyz and the kennetek generator's README) — and the community norm is free: mature parametric generators cover any bin size at no cost. Verified paid demand for Gridfinity *files* exists on exactly one of the six platforms checked: **Etsy**, where 13 priced digital listings were verified, mostly niche bundles (kitchen, tools) at $2–$13, heavily discounted, with no sales counts visible. Everywhere else, money moves indirectly: MakerWorld sells **commercial licences** for free models, Cults has pay-what-you-want, Thangs sells platform-wide memberships, and the only genuinely productised paid offerings are off-platform generator services (Tracemate photo-trace cutouts, €4.99–€19). No paid Gridfinity listing could be verified on the Printables Store or on Gumroad at all. A paid pack can probably sell, but only as curated convenience — niche-specific, complete, print-tested sets — not as generic bins, which free generators already give away parametrically.

### MakerWorld — models are free; the paid layer is commercial licences

MakerWorld's own FAQ states "all of MakerWorld's models are free and not behind paywalls." What designers sell is a **Commercial License Membership** — the right to sell *physical prints* of their free models (platform-wide price range $3–$300/month per Bambu's blog; the specific tier price sits behind each page's Join button and was **not shown** in the fetched text). Verified Gridfinity examples:

## 2. The free alternatives a paid pack must beat

The bar is high and entirely free. The original system is MIT-licensed, and parametric generation — the thing a file pack would seem to sell — is already solved several times over:

- **The originals** — Zack Freedman's bins and baseplates (incl. his parametric Fusion 360 source), catalogued at https://gridfinity.xyz. License: MIT per the wiki and the kennetek README. (Wikipedia's Gridfinity article claims a NonCommercial Creative Commons license for both Gridfinity and its inspiration, Alexandre Chappel's Assortment System; the primary/community sources consistently say MIT for Gridfinity itself and CC-BY-NC-SA for Chappel's system. Either way, a paid pack cannot lean on the base system being closed.)
- **gridfinity-rebuilt-openscad** (kennetek, GitHub) — ground-up OpenSCAD rebuild: any width/length/height, compartments, scoop, tabs, magnet/screw holes, stacking lip, vase-mode bins, baseplates. **2,282 stars, 331 forks** as shown. MIT. https://github.com/kennetek/gridfinity-rebuilt-openscad
- **More OpenSCAD libraries** — gridfinity_openscad (Jamie/vector76, MIT) and gridfinity_extended_openscad (Chris Heazlewood, GPL since Dec 2024), catalogued at https://gridfinity.tools.
- **Free web generators (no install)** — Jamie's browser OpenSCAD Customizer; Perplexing Labs' Gridfinity Generator (bins, baseplates, lids) at https://gridfinity.perplexinglabs.com; Gridfinity Creator (Jeroen Bouwens — exports **STEP** as well as STL) at https://gridfinity.bouwens.co; a photo-outline tool (Georgs Lazdāns) that traces cutouts from a photograph; Gridfinity Rebase (Timothy J. Aveni), which swaps any downloaded bin's base/magnet style automatically. Also catalogued on gridfinity.xyz: gridfinitygenerator.com, Extrabold.tools, tooltrace.ai, plus drawer-layout planners.
- **Pre-generated free packs** — MakerWorld model 25563: Zack-remix bins with full-width label tabs, **pre-generated STLs 1×1–7×7 in 3U/4U/5U/6U for both 6×2 mm and 6×3 mm magnets**, plus a redone parametric Fusion file working 1×1–10×10. https://makerworld.com/en/models/25563
- **Pred's Gridfinity Storage Box** (Printables 543553) — parametric lidded box, free, **rated 4.89/5 from 547 ratings with 1,112 reviews** as shown. https://www.printables.com/model/543553-gridfinity-storage-box-by-pred-now-parametric
- **Gridfinity Specification + Refined** (grizzie17, Printables 417152 / 413761) — the community spec and an enhanced implementation (press-fit baseplate magnets, side-insert bin magnets, expandable baseplates), free.

Takeaway: any size of plain bin, baseplate, or lid is free in parametric form, and the popular fixed packs are free too. What free does *not* provide is curation for a specific job (a GEDORE socket set, a watercolor desk), verified print profiles, and the fiddly features in section 3.

## 3. Gaps — what buyers and users ask for that nobody sells well

Five, with the comments quoted (public usernames as shown on the pages):

1. **Latches and hinges that actually work in PETG.** On Printables' flagship free storage box, user Anne A Thomas: *"love the box but cannot get the latches to stay in. What is the secret to seating them?"* And user Kirtle: *"I just couldn't get these hinges to print properly at all in PETG, it's probably user error but even with brims they would just topple while printing and create spaghetti. I caved in and printed the hinges in PLA, but definitely the weakest point of failure in mine (and in others, from what I read in the comments)."* The designer himself warns tolerances are tight and tabs may need filing. A paid box system with print-tested, PETG-reliable hardware would answer the most visible pain on the most popular Gridfinity box. https://www.printables.com/model/543553-gridfinity-storage-box-by-pred-now-parametric/comments
2. **Lids for small compartment bins, inside the generators.** Onshape forum, on the Gridfinity bin/base generator discussion: *"Hello, So I have no idea how to work with the feature scripts on onshape, but would it be possible to add lid functionality like what's designed here? I want to add lids to compartment bins in a 1ux1u bin on a larger 4x3u bin for hook storage."* Standalone boxes have lids; compartment-bin lids are not a standard generator feature. (Quote captured via search-engine capture of the discussion.) https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/comment/116251
3. **Click-in labels.** Same Onshape discussion: *"Can we please get an option for clicky labels?"* Labelling is a persistent weak spot — the free ecosystem offers label tabs and a separate label generator, but snap-in labelling as a built-in generator option is what users ask for by name. (Search-captured quote, as above.)
4. **Small mechanical completeness in "finished" products.** Thangs, under-desk Gridfinity drawer, user John H: *"any reason there isnt a stop to keep the 06 pin from rotating clockwise? there is one to prevent it rotating counter clockwise. I had a minor issue when i didnt tighten it quite enough and it was rotating in there… a stop in the other direction seems like it might help in case the plastic screw gets a little loose."* (The designer replied he'll add it to the next version.) Buyers of finished designs expect these details finished. https://thangs.com/designer/Matthew%20Ghost/3d-model/Modular%20Under%20Desk%20Drawer%20%7C%204%C3%972%20Gridfinity%20%7C%20Push-Push%20Lock-1573252
5. **The sizes and features the big free collections still miss.** Designer-relayed demand on MakerWorld's Card Trays page: *"Other requests have come from the comments including adding magnet holes on the bottom."* (shipped in v2), and on the large Bin Collection page the designer offers *"If you need another size of bin send me a msg :)"* — even a 419-review collection doesn't cover the sizes people need. Handles tell the same story: Pred's box description admits of its front handle, *"I don't like the handle, but that was a commonly requested feature."* Half-unit/gap-filler bins are the recognised version of this gap: odd drawer remainders are exactly what the standard grid doesn't fill. https://makerworld.com/en/models/1535647-gridfinity-card-trays-for-tcg-storage · https://makerworld.com/en/models/579916-gridfinity-bin-collection

## 4. Platform fees and payouts (official pages)

- **Printables Store (Prusa)** — Creators T&Cs (https://www.prusa3d.com/page/printables-club-and-store-terms-and-conditions-for-creators_236503/): platform fee **20% of each download payment, 10% of Club subscription payments** (§4.2). All payments run through **Stripe directly to the creator's account — Prusa never holds the money** (§4.3); Stripe's own fee is on top (T&C example: $10 sale → $1 Printables fee + $0.59 Stripe → creator $8.41). No Prusa payout threshold (Stripe's schedule applies); no seller subscription fee stated; selling needs an approved store registration + verified Stripe account.
- **MakerWorld (Bambu Lab)** — **no paid-download storefront exists** (FAQ: models "are free and not behind paywalls"; https://makerworld.com/en/faq). Monetisation: (a) Points/Boosts — regular Points have **no cash value**; **Exclusive Points** (Exclusive Model Program, https://makerworld.com/en/exclusive-model-policy) are worth **$0.066 each**, cashable only at a **$100 minimum** after a 14-day review, and exclusivity requires removing the model from all other platforms; (b) **Commercial License Membership**: platform fee **10%**, paid via **PayPal only**, **31-day hold**, creator sets $3–$300/month; eligibility ≥200 followers and ≥600 prints.
- **Cults3D** — official blog (https://cults3d.com/en/blog/articles/how-to-make-money-with-3d-printer): designer earns **80%**; Cults' **20% commission is presented as all-in** (covers ~5% bank charges, hosting, bandwidth). **No subscription** to start selling. Payout threshold/method: **not stated** on the official pages consulted.
- **Thangs** — official sell page (https://thangs.com/content/sell-3d-printed-products-and-designs/): individual model sales fee is tiered by price — **30% under $5, 20% from $5 to under $10, 15% at $10+** — plus payment-processor and currency-conversion fees. Memberships: **14%** + processor fees. Bundle program: designers share **50% of the subscription fee** among those a subscriber downloaded from. Payouts via **Stripe** (seller must enable it); payout threshold **not stated** on the sell page (deferred to the T&Cs); no membership cost to sell stated.
- **Etsy** — official Help Center (https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115014483627-What-are-the-Fees-and-Taxes-for-Selling-on-Etsy): listing fee **$0.20** per listing (4-month expiry, auto-renews); transaction fee **6.5%** of the total; Etsy Payments processing **US: 3% + $0.25**; one-time shop set-up fee (amount varies, shown during setup); **Offsite Ads 15%** (under $10k trailing sales, opt-out allowed) or **12%** (≥$10k, mandatory), capped at $100/order; currency conversion 2.5% where applicable; **US has no deposit minimum** in the official table.
- **Gumroad** — official Help Center (https://gumroad.com/help/article/66-gumroads-fees.html · https://gumroad.com/help/article/13-getting-paid): direct sales **10% + $0.50** per transaction *plus* processing (cards 2.9% + $0.30; PayPal fees vary); **Discover marketplace sales flat 30% all-in**; months over $20,000 in sales drop new direct sales to **5% + $0.50**; no monthly fee. Payouts need a **$100 minimum balance** (note: third-party pages still quote $10 — the current official article says $100); bank deposit or PayPal (PayPal payouts carry a 2% fee); US instant payouts at 3% with no $100 minimum.

Worked comparison for a $9.99 pack sold direct: Printables ≈ $7.40 after 20% + Stripe (per the T&C's own math pattern); Cults $7.99 (80%, all-in per Cults); Thangs $8.49 before processor fees (15% tier); Etsy ≈ $8.71 before the $0.20 listing fee and any Offsite Ads cut; Gumroad ≈ $8.20 after 10% + $0.50 + card processing — but its Discover traffic costs a flat 30%.

## How I checked it

- Every listing, fee figure and quote above comes from a page opened and read on 2026-10-17 (platform listing/market pages, official terms/help/sell pages, model comment sections). Counts, ratings and prices are reported exactly as shown; where a page did not show a field, the report says "not shown" rather than estimating.
- Etsy: Etsy's search endpoint returns 403 to a text fetcher, so listings were verified on Etsy's own market pages (which show title + price). One full individual listing page was opened to confirm listing pages render licence text, but that product wasn't Gridfinity and was excluded.
- Negative results were checked, not assumed: Printables models were checked via their embedded price data (~10 models, all $0), Gumroad via four differently-phrased searches, Cults via its tag page and a designer's full catalogue, Thangs via search plus an opened model page.
- Fees come only from official pages: Prusa's Creator T&Cs, MakerWorld's FAQ and Exclusive Model Program agreement, Cults' official blog, Thangs' official sell page, Etsy's Help Center and Gumroad's Help Center. Where an official page was silent (Cults and Thangs payout thresholds), the report says so instead of using third-party summaries.
- The Gridfinity license point was cross-checked across the community wiki, the kennetek README and MakerWorld descriptions; the conflicting Wikipedia claim is flagged in the text.
