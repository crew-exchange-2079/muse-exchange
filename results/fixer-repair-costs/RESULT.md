# Fixer-upper repair costs: north-central West Virginia, 2026 — result

Summary: follow-up to `results/fixer-upper-research`, for a 1,500+ sq ft house built roughly 1940–1990 in Harrison, Marion, Monongalia, Lewis, Upshur and nearby counties. The headline adjustment is smaller than most buyers expect: construction cost indexes put north-central WV only about **4–5% below the US national average in total** (labour ~9% below, materials essentially at national prices), so the table below takes published national ranges and adjusts them by roughly 5%, rounded — it does **not** apply a fantasy "WV discount". The genuinely dangerous money in these houses is concentrated in a short list: failed septic/drain field, bowing foundation walls, a failed sewer lateral, end-of-life roof plus a dead HVAC system in the same house, and whole-house lead/asbestos abatement. Almost everything else is a routine, priceable repair. All figures are 2026 planning ranges in USD, retrieved 2026-10-05, and are not quotes — the softest numbers (PEX repipe, septic replacement, lead/asbestos) are flagged where they appear and in "Unsure about".

Notes on the table:
- Furnace and AC done **together** in one visit usually cost less than the two separate rows added up (one service visit, one permit set) — the earlier brief's combined range was $5,000–$12,500 nationally.
- The two widest-error rows are the **PEX repipe** (published national estimates for the same 1,500 sq ft house ran from about $2,300 to about $6,750 depending on source — drywall cutting/patching is often extra) and **septic replacement** (soil and drain-field scope move it more than any other item here).
- Foundation "typical" assumes one wall stabilised (anchors run roughly $400–$700 each; carbon-fibre straps $350–$1,000 each) **or** a few piers (push piers roughly $1,000–$2,500 each; helical $2,000–$4,000 each). A wall bowed more than about 2 inches, or several walls moving, leaves the "typical" band quickly.

## 2. Regional adjustments — WV vs national

- **The index evidence.** RSMeans/Gordian City Cost Indexes express each city's costs as a factor of the US national average (= 100). For the north-central WV cities in the published table: **Clarksburg total 95.7** (materials 99.3, installation/labour 91.0), **Morgantown 95.9**, **Buckhannon 95.1** — i.e. total building costs about **4–5% below national**, made up of **materials at ~95–100% of national (essentially no discount)** and **labour about 9% below national** (the WV range across all listed cities is roughly 8–19% below on labour; Beckley total 91.9 and Martinsburg 89.7 are the low outliers, neither in the target counties). There is no published index for Fairmont or Philippi; Clarksburg and Buckhannon are the nearest proxies. Caveat: the freely published RSMeans file is a 2021 update and current data is paywalled — the *pattern* (small total discount, all of it in labour) is the durable part, not the decimal.
- **Corroboration.** The Bureau of Economic Analysis 2024 Regional Price Parity for West Virginia is 89.5 overall (US = 100; goods component 96.5) — general prices about 10% below national, with goods much closer to national, consistent with the RSMeans picture. A secondary building-cost estimating site puts WV home building costs about 5% below national (methodology unstated — indicative only).
- **How the table uses this.** Published national ranges were adjusted down ~5% on the total and rounded to planning numbers. Two practical warnings: (1) materials-heavy jobs (windows, appliances, fixtures) get almost **no** WV discount, because materials price nationally; labour-heavy jobs (paint, rewire, excavation) get more. (2) The index assumes a contractor is available — in Lewis/Upshur/Barbour, scarce licensed trades and rural travel time can erase the labour discount entirely on a specific job. Get two or three written local quotes during the inspection period; on a bones item, the quote *is* the number.

## 3. Deal-killers vs fixables

"Deal-killer" here means: at the $100,000–$175,000 target from the earlier brief, the repair usually cannot be priced into the deal and still leave a working house — walk away, or buy only at land/estate pricing with the full quote already in hand.

**Usually deal-killers**
- **Failed septic with a failed perc / no legal place to put a new field.** A $14,000 typical replacement is priceable; "the lot cannot support a compliant system" is not a price, it is a dead end. Same logic for a sewer lateral that must cross a state road or a neighbour's property with no easement.
- **Foundation movement on more than one wall, or a bow beyond ~2 inches with floor/joist damage.** One stabilised wall is a repair; a house still moving on two sides is an engineering project with an open-ended end.
- **Mine subsidence damage already in motion** (see section 4) — cracking that is active and unremediated, especially where cover was waived by a previous owner.
- **Whole-house lead + asbestos abatement as a condition of occupancy or insurance** (both present, both disturbed-scope): each is manageable scoped; together, whole-house, they can consume the entire equity cushion before any visible improvement exists.
- **A bones pile-up in one house:** end-of-life roof **and** dead HVAC **and** a panel/rewire **and** a water problem. Individually routine; together they typically exceed what the discount on a fixer can carry — run the house-by-house arithmetic (renovated value minus price, repairs, 20–30% contingency, holding/selling costs) rather than a slogan.
- **Uninsurable as it stands** (panel type, roof age, plumbing material, knob-and-tube) where the seller will not allow the fix or the lender requires it before closing and nobody can pay for it pre-closing. An uninsurable house is an unsellable house.

**Routine fixables (price them, don't fear them)**
- Water heater, well pump, furnace/AC at end of life with working distribution, panel upgrade, galvanised plumbing with decent pressure today (repipe later, in phases), single-wall foundation stabilisation with an engineer's letter, interior waterproofing where the real problem is gutters/grading, radon (a ~$1,600 system is one of the cheapest certainties on the list), windows one elevation at a time, and every cosmetic item. Mould belongs here **only** when its water source is found and fixed with it; mould with an unfound source is a deal-killer wearing a fixable's clothes.

## 4. Inspection add-ons worth paying for

Base point: on a 1940–1990 WV house the general inspection is triage. These add-ons are the diagnosis, and all of them together cost less than 1% of a typical purchase price.

| Add-on | Typical cost | When it is worth it (which is: almost always, here) |
|---|---|---|
| Sewer camera scope | ~$100–$350 (weakest sourcing of this set — no major aggregator page found; Realtor.com citing InterNACHI gives $100–$250) | Any house on public sewer with cast-iron/Orangeburg-era drains, slow lowest drains, or big trees over the line. Replaces a possible $5,500 surprise with a fact |
| Radon test (professional, continuous monitor) | ~$150–$400 (DIY kits $10–$50 for screening) | Every purchase in this region. The result is negotiable currency and sizes the ~$900–$3,000 mitigation decision |
| Structural engineer (inspection + written report) | ~$300–$1,000; foundation-focused inspection/report often $250–$500 | Any bowing, horizontal cracking, sloping floors, or a seller's "it has always been like that". The letter also unlocks contractor quotes and lender/insurer acceptance |
| Septic evaluation (and pump-out if due) | A few hundred dollars — **low confidence**: no solid published range found in this pass; confirm the price when booking | Any house on septic with unknown age/records. Pair it with county health-department records, which are cheap or free and often hold the permit history |
| Lead / asbestos testing | Lead test ~$250–$700; asbestos testing ~$230–$780 | Pre-1978 houses (lead) and any house where pipe wrap, tile, siding or textured coatings will be disturbed by the renovation plan |
| Mine subsidence — not a test, an insurance check | Premium is tens of dollars a year (a consumer comparison lists ~$48/yr for WV — secondary; the FAIR Plan's approved 2021 rating rules priced dwelling cover from $5/yr at ≤$10,000 of cover to $23/yr in the $95,001–$100,000 band) | See below |

**WV mine subsidence insurance — how it works and when it matters.** Coverage for loss from collapse of man-made underground coal mines sits in a state fund run by the Board of Risk and Insurance Management (BRIM) under WV Code §33-30, and it reaches a homeowner through the ordinary homeowners policy: **every policy insuring a structure in WV must include subsidence coverage at a separately stated premium unless the owner waives it in writing**. Harrison, Marion and Barbour are *not* on the statutory exception list, so in the target counties the default is included-unless-waived. The fund reinsures up to **$200,000 per structure**, with a statutory deductible of **$250–$500**; "structure" expressly includes foundations, footings, basements, septic systems and underground pipes. Two traps: new coverage takes effect on the **30th calendar day after application**, so a buyer who adds it at closing has a month uninsured; and $200,000 may be well below replacement cost on a fully renovated house. Practical actions at purchase: do not sign the waiver, confirm the separately stated subsidence premium is on the declarations page, and ask whether the property sits over or near mapped workings. Premiums are set by the board and no current official consumer premium schedule was found — treat any specific dollar figure, including the ~$48 above, as indicative until it is on your own quote.

## 5. Financing for repairs

**FHA 203(k)** — the main purchase-plus-rehab mortgage (details verified in the earlier brief).
- *Limited:* up to **$75,000** of rehab (raised from $35,000 by HUD Mortgagee Letter 2024-13, Nov 2024), no minimum, non-structural work only, house must be habitable, **no HUD consultant required**, ~2 draws over up to 9 months. 3.5% down at 580+ credit.
- *Standard:* minimum $5,000 rehab, no separate rehab cap, structural work and uninhabitable houses allowed, **HUD-approved consultant required**, up to 5 draws over 12 months.
- Both: repair money sits in lender escrow and is paid to a **licensed contractor** in inspected draws — DIY is generally barred; closings run ~45–60 days (Limited) and ~60–90 days (Standard). The 2026 FHA 1-unit limit for Harrison/Marion/Barbour is $541,287 — not binding at this price point.

**Fannie Mae HomeStyle Renovation (conventional)** — rehab up to **75% of the lesser of (purchase + rehab) or as-completed value**; as low as 3% down on a 1-unit primary residence, ~620 credit, complete within 12 months, same escrow/draw/licensed-contractor mechanics. Unlike FHA: second homes and 1-unit investment properties are eligible, and PMI falls away at 20% equity. Fewer local lenders offer it — confirm a lender actually closes HomeStyle in these counties before relying on it.

**West Virginia programmes (WVHDF) — the honest headline: there is no WVHDF purchase-plus-renovation mortgage.** WVHDF's official single-family list is four products (Homeownership Program, Movin' Up, Low Down Home Loan, Residential Septic Loan Program), and none of them rolls repair costs into a purchase loan the way 203(k) does. What they can do:
- *Homeownership Program* (lower-income first-time buyers): 30-year fixed loans financing **up to 100% of the purchase price** — rate shown on the programme page at retrieval was 5.790% (rates move with bond issues; check the page). The house still has to pass appraisal and insurer requirements as a purchase.
- *Movin' Up:* moderate-income, first-time *or repeat* buyers (rate shown at retrieval 7.500%).
- *Low Down Home Loan:* down-payment/closing-cost help as a second loan, only alongside a WVHDF first mortgage — **up to $12,000 at 2% fixed, 15 years** (the maximum was raised from $8,000 in Jan 2026; older guides quoting $8,000 are stale).
- *Residential Septic Loan Program* (with WV DEP): **existing homeowners only** — the home cannot be on the market when applying, so it cannot fix a septic to enable a purchase. WVHDF training materials describe terms of generally up to **$10,000 at 2% fixed, max 10 years** (the programme page itself does not state dollar terms); the contractor is paid after the county sanitarian signs off.
- Practical WV stack, therefore: buy with a WVHDF (or plain) mortgage if the house passes as-is → phase repairs from cash → use the septic loan and/or USDA 504 (below) after you own it. If the house cannot pass a plain appraisal, 203(k) / HomeStyle are the routes, not WVHDF.

**USDA Section 504 (owners only, not a purchase loan)** — current official terms: maximum **loan $40,000**, maximum **grant $10,000**, combined $50,000; loan at **1% fixed over 20 years**; grants only at age 62+ and only to remove health/safety hazards, repayable if the home is sold within 3 years. Eligibility: very-low income for the county, own and occupy, rural-area property, unable to get affordable credit elsewhere. Older pages quoting $20,000 / $7,500 are outdated. USDA *purchase* loans (502) are a separate thing — 0% down for eligible rural addresses, but the house must meet property standards as-is and there is no rehab budget; eligibility is by exact address.

## Unsure about

- **No WV-specific contractor price book exists** in the sources found. Every table figure is a national range adjusted ~5% per the index evidence; on any specific house, two or three written local quotes replace this table.
- **Softest numbers:** PEX repipe (published estimates for the same house size spread roughly $2,300–$6,750), full septic replacement ($7,000–$40,000+, driven by soil/perc and whether the field must move), sewer scope price (no major aggregator page found), septic evaluation price (no solid published range found), and lead/asbestos project totals (per-square-foot rates are solid; whole-house totals depend entirely on how much material is in scope).
- **RSMeans index age:** the freely published location factors are a 2021 update. The labour/materials split pattern is consistent with BEA 2024 data, but the exact 95.7 for Clarksburg should not be treated as a 2026-precise figure.
- **WVHDF rates** shown on programme pages (5.790% / 7.500%) were the rates displayed on the retrieval date and change with bond issues; the RSLP dollar terms (up to ~$10,000, 2%, 10 years) come from WVHDF training materials, not the programme page itself.
- **DIY flags** describe practical capability, not legal permission: permit and licensing rules vary by county and trade, and any loan with escrowed repairs (203(k), HomeStyle) generally requires licensed contractors regardless.
