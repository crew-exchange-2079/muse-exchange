# What small independent IT providers publicly charge for setup-and-support packages (1–15 staff)

Scope: published prices for setup-and-support packages aimed at very small businesses — a restaurant, brewpub, inn or shop with 1–15 staff. All prices in USD, all read 2026-11-17. Every price below was on a public page; nothing came from a login, a quote request, a purchase or a message to anyone.

Headline finding: the market splits three ways, and the split matters more than any single number. (1) Per-user / per-computer managed IT from small MSPs: **$35–$300 per user or computer per month, median $125**. (2) Per-location restaurant support: **$79–$379 per location per month**. (3) One-off freelance gigs (Upwork/Fiverr): **$10–$375 per job**, no monthly cover. For a restaurant, brewpub or inn, per-user pricing is usually the wrong meter — most staff never touch a managed computer — so per-device or per-location pricing is normally far cheaper than the per-user headline suggests.

## 2. Medians and the 1–5 person range

### Median monthly price

I took one headline monthly rate per named plan from the per-user / per-computer / per-workstation / per-endpoint rows above (rows 1–19, 26–28, excluding per-location restaurant plans and one-off gigs, which are priced on a different meter). The 27 values, sorted, are:

$35, $45, $55, $55, $75, $75, $75, $75, $80, $95, $99, $120, $125, **$125**, $125, $125, $125, $125, $149, $150, $150, $150, $195, $199, $200, $249, $300

- **Median monthly price: $125 per user / computer / workstation / endpoint per month** (14th of 27 values).
- Range of those headline rates: $35–$300. Interquartile spread: roughly $75 (25th percentile) to $150 (75th percentile).
- The median is a per-unit rate, not a total bill. A 5-person shop at the median pays about $625/mo; a 15-person shop about $1,875/mo — before add-ons, licences and servers.
- Cross-check: independent 2026 pricing guides put the same market at $100–$200 per user per month for most small businesses, $150–$175 typical for a 10–50 person office (https://www.edconusa.com/blog/managed-it-services-cost-small-business, read 2026-11-17; https://aliteq.com/managed-it-services-pricing, read 2026-11-17). The $125 median from published rate cards sits inside that guide range, at its lower end — consistent with small-town and independent providers pricing below metro MSPs.

### Median one-time setup price

Only four hard setup figures are actually published by providers in the table: $0 (CRC Cloud onboarding), $99 (OH Sweet Hospitality, per module), $250 (KregerTech, per 10 computers) and $1,000 (OH Sweet Hospitality standard onboarding — waived for founders).

- **Median of published setup prices: $174.50** (average of the middle two, $99 and $250).
- Treat this median as weak: it rests on 4 data points, and most providers publish no setup fee at all. The honest picture is that setup is usually quoted, not published. Market guides put typical MSP onboarding at **$1,000–$5,000** (https://joyvara.com/blog/managed-it-services-pricing-2026/, read 2026-11-17) or **1–3x the monthly rate** (https://www.edconusa.com/blog/managed-it-services-cost-small-business, read 2026-11-17). At the $125 median monthly rate, 1–3x monthly implies roughly $125–$375 setup per user for a small shop — far below the $1,000–$5,000 guide figure, which reflects larger offices. Unsure which applies to a 1–5 person shop; see "Unsure about".

## 4. Three common complaints owners post about their IT provider — and what a new provider should promise to avoid

These are complaints as owners and trade writers describe them, with links. Note: most of the detailed write-ups are by IT providers describing what they hear from switchers, not verbatim owner posts — Reddit searches for first-person owner complaints on r/smallbusiness and r/msp returned no usable threads on 2026-11-17, so I am not presenting these as direct owner quotes. Each complaint below is paired with the promise a new provider should make in writing.

### Complaint 1 — Slow response times: tickets sit for hours or days, and nobody calls back

- "A slow response is the most common reason companies lose trust in their IT provider. Employees send a ticket or make a call, then wait far too long for help. Work stops. Customers wait." — AIS, https://www.ais-now.com/blog/7-most-common-complaints-it-support-companies-how-to-avoid, read 2026-11-17.
- "Calls and tickets sit for days, or you have to chase to get an answer… A server that is down for a morning with no callback is not acceptable, and neither is a week of silence on anything." — Cross River Technology, https://www.crossrivertechnology.com/insights/signs-you-need-to-change-your-managed-service-provider/, read 2026-11-17.
- "It starts small. A ticket that used to get answered in an hour now takes half a day. Then a full day. Nobody announces the change. It just creeps." — VJNetworks, https://vjnetworks.com/signs-you-need-a-new-it-company/, read 2026-11-17.
- Same pattern reported by E-N Computers: "Responses that used to take a few hours now take a day or more… your bills are cryptic, late, or both" — https://www.encomputers.com/2026/06/signs-it-provider-failing/, read 2026-11-17.

**Promise to make:** a written response-time SLA with numbers, not adjectives — e.g. a human response within 15 minutes for routine issues and 1–4 hours for follow-up depending on severity (the bar E-N Computers cites), or same-day response on every ticket in writing (Other Guys IT, https://www.otherguys.tech/, read 2026-11-17). Publish the response targets on the pricing page, as Powerful IT Systems does (5/15/30 minutes by priority, https://www.powerful-it.com/pricing/, read 2026-11-17).

### Complaint 2 — Surprise fees and unpredictable invoices: the cheap monthly price covers only a fraction of the work

- "Many businesses choose an inexpensive IT provider and later discover that the low price covers only a fraction of what they need. Everything else becomes an extra charge. Basic support might be included, but on-site visits, after-hours help, device setup, security upgrades, or backup improvements all cost more." — AIS, https://www.ais-now.com/blog/7-most-common-complaints-it-support-companies-how-to-avoid, read 2026-11-17.
- "If you are suddenly being billed for hidden or unexplained costs you were never told about, this is a key indicator that your MSP isn't being level with you." — Kelser Corporation, https://www.kelsercorp.com/blog/signs-to-switch-managed-it-services-providers, read 2026-11-17.

- The scale of the extras is itemised in Joyvara's hidden-fee table: per-incident charges $75–$200 per ticket on top of monthly, "exceeded scope" charges, early termination fees of 3–12 months remaining, data migration/return fees of $500–$5,000 — https://joyvara.com/blog/managed-it-services-pricing-2026/, read 2026-11-17.

**Promise to make:** one flat published price with a written list of what is extra and what each extra costs, before signing — the KregerTech/CRC Cloud model of publishing the whole rate card. No per-ticket charges on a managed plan, no "exceeded scope" billing without a written quote first, and itemised invoices.

### Complaint 3 — Locked in: the provider holds the passwords, the documentation and the contract, so the owner cannot leave

- "Do you feel trapped with your current provider, even if you're unhappy? This 'vendor lock-in' happens when switching seems too difficult or costly. Reasons might include inflexible long-term contracts, reliance on proprietary tools, or even the provider making it hard to access your own critical information, like network passwords. Holding passwords hostage is unethical, but subtle lock-in is common." — Apex Technology, https://apextechcorp.com/blog/is-your-charlotte-it-provider-quietly-holding-your-business-hostage-6-red-flags-you-can-t-ignore, read 2026-11-17.

**Promise to make:** the client owns all admin accounts, passwords, documentation and data from day one, in a portal the client can access any time; month-to-month terms with 30 days' notice and no early-termination fee; free handover of documentation, credentials and the latest restore point on exit — exactly as CRC Cloud publishes ("If you ever leave — standard handover… $0", https://www.crccloud.com/pricing/, read 2026-11-17) and as KregerTech ("month-to-month with 30 days' notice", https://kregertech.com/pricing/, read 2026-11-17) and CMIT Northern Shenandoah Valley ("no long-term lock-in… no early termination penalties", https://cmitsolutions.com/shenandoah-va-1096/it-service-packages/, read 2026-11-17) state.

## What this means for pricing a starter package

- Anchor the headline near the published median: **about $125 per user/computer per month** for a full managed package, or **$75–$150** for a leaner one. The cheapest credible published entry point is $35/user/mo (help-desk-only, Corporate Technologies); the cheapest full-service per-computer rate is $55–$75 (EZETech, Powerful IT, CMIT Core).
- For a restaurant/brewpub/inn, sell **per location or per device, not per user**: $79–$200 per location per month for remote support (Science On Call, Tradewind) is the directly comparable market, and it undercuts per-user pricing by a wide margin when 15 staff share 3 computers.
- Publish the setup fee. Almost nobody else does, and its absence is itself a complaint driver (see complaint 2). KregerTech's $250 per 10 computers and CRC Cloud's $0 onboarding are the only clean published examples; a $150–$250 setup for a 1–5 person shop is defensible and sits at the median of what is published.
- Price the hospitality extras separately and name them: backup internet ($49/location/mo), guest Wi-Fi ($39/location/mo), menu service ($59/location/mo), PCI log history ($5/user/mo), AI chatbot ($49/mo self-serve). These are the extras hospitality owners actually buy, and each has a published price above.
- Put the three promises in writing on the pricing page: a numeric response SLA, a flat price with named extras, and client ownership of all credentials plus month-to-month terms. Those three promises answer the three most repeated complaints verbatim.
