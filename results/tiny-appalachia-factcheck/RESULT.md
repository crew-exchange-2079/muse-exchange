# Fact-check: five Tiny Appalachia captions

*Checked 2026-10-06 against primary/official sources only (WV State Parks, National Park Service, US Forest Service, the railroad operator, and the award's original publisher). Verdicts use the scale: correct / roughly right / wrong / unverifiable.*

## 1. Glade Creek Grist Mill, Babcock State Park
Caption: "West Virginia's most photographed place — but make it miniature."
- **Claim:** the mill/park is West Virginia's most photographed place.
- **Verdict: roughly right** — the official claim is hedged, not a superlative. WV State Parks' own wording is: "It's the Glade Creek Grist Mill — one of the most photographed images in the world." The same page quotes the miller calling it "the most photographed mill in the United States" — a person's quote about the *mill*, not an official claim about a "place." No official page found uses the caption's wording.
- **Source:** [WV State Parks — Glade Creek Grist Mill](https://wvstateparks.com/glade-creek-grist-mill-babcock/)
- **Corrected caption:** "One of the most photographed places in West Virginia — but make it miniature."

## 2. Harpers Ferry
Caption: "Two rivers, one tiny town, 200 years of history in 6 seconds."
- **Claims:** (a) two rivers meet at the town; (b) it has 200 years of history.
- **Verdict: roughly right** — the rivers are right, the year count is understated. NPS: "On the left lies the Potomac River and to the right lies the Shenandoah River… one can see how the Shenandoah and Potomac gracefully come together." On age, NPS records that Robert Harper "obtained a patent for 125 acres in 1751" and that "in 1763, the Virginia General Assembly established the town of 'Shenandoah Falls at Mr. Harper's Ferry'" — so the town is 263 years old in 2026 (≈279 years counting the ferry service from ~1747). "200 years" is safe but sells it short.
- **Sources:** [NPS — Rivers and Streams](https://nps.gov/hafe/learn/nature/rivers.htm); [NPS — Robert Harper](http://www.nps.gov/hafe/historyculture/robert-harper.htm)
- **Corrected caption:** "Two rivers, one tiny town, 260+ years of history in 6 seconds."

## 3. Cass Scenic Railroad
Caption: "A 1905 steam locomotive, shrunk. The whistle is real-sized."
- **Claim:** a locomotive built in 1905 runs at Cass today.
- **Verdict: correct.** The operator, Durbin & Greenbrier Valley Railroad, says of Shay No. 5: "Delivered in November 1905 for the original Cass-based railroad… No. 5 is the oldest and longest-operating Shay at Cass," and "the 5 is the oldest operational Shay in the world and the official steam locomotive of West Virginia!" Its spec table gives "Built: 11-11-1905." The builder (Lima Locomotive Works) is confirmed in the WV Legislature's resolution designating No. 5 the state steam locomotive. (Note: Cass's roster also holds a second 1905 Shay, No. 1 — but No. 5 is the one in service.)
- **Source:** [Mountain Rail WV (operator) — Shay 5](https://mountainrailwv.com/shay-5/)
- No correction needed.

## 4. Lewisburg
Caption: "America's coolest small town, pocket-sized."
- **Claim:** Lewisburg was named America's coolest small town.
- **Verdict: correct.** The title came from Budget Travel magazine's reader-vote contest "Coolest Small Towns in America." The Greenbrier County CVB states it verbatim: Lewisburg was "named it 'America's Coolest Small Town' by Budget Travel magazine in 2011." The Associated Press reported on Feb. 13, 2011 that "Budget Travel says that Lewisburg came in first in online voting" (437,000+ votes). Caveat: Budget Travel's original article page no longer appears on budgettravel.com, so the title wording and year rest on official corroboration (county CVB, Visit Southern West Virginia) plus the AP report, not the magazine's own surviving page.
- **Sources:** [Greenbrier County CVB](https://greenbrierwv.com/press-room/dine-like-a-local-in-lewisburg); [Visit Southern West Virginia](https://visitwv.com/blog-post/wv-cool-small-towns/)
- No correction needed.

## 5. Seneca Rocks
Caption: "900 feet of stone, now fits in your hand."
- **Claim:** Seneca Rocks is 900 feet of stone.
- **Verdict: correct.** The US Forest Service (Monongahela National Forest) says: "The rocks are a magnificent formation rising nearly 900 feet above the North Fork River," and identifies the rock as Tuscarora sandstone ("Due to the hardness of the Tuscarora sandstone formation…"). "900 feet" matches the official figure (strictly, "nearly 900").
- **Source:** [USFS — Seneca Rocks recreation area](https://www.fs.usda.gov/recarea/mnf/recarea/?recid=7051)
- No correction needed.

## How I checked it

Each caption was checked on the official page itself, not a snippet or blog: WV State Parks' mill feature page for Babcock; NPS Harpers Ferry pages for the rivers and the town's founding dates (with the year count computed from NPS's own 1763 date); the railroad operator's own Shay No. 5 page for the Cass build year and operating status; Budget Travel corroboration via the Greenbrier County CVB and a contemporaneous AP report for Lewisburg; and the US Forest Service's Seneca Rocks recreation page for height and rock type. Quotes above are short verbatim excerpts from those pages.

## Unsure about

- WV Tourism's wording for Babcock could not be confirmed — no wvtourism.com / gotowv.com page making a "most photographed" claim surfaced, so verdict 1 rests on the WV State Parks page alone.
- Budget Travel's original 2011 article appears to be gone from its website; the Lewisburg title and year are confirmed by the county CVB and AP reporting rather than the magazine directly.
- The ~1747 start for Harper's ferry service comes from NPS's timeline page; the NPS biography page confirms 1751 (land patent) and 1763 (town established) verbatim. Either date makes "200 years" an understatement, so the verdict doesn't turn on it.
