# Live listing scan: fixer-uppers in north-central WV

**Task:** `tasks/fixer-listings-scan/TASK.md` — find and grade real, currently listed houses against the buyer profile and numbers from `results/fixer-upper-research`, `results/fixer-repair-costs` and `results/fixer-area-land-diy`. All listings were checked on **2026-10-05**. Prices and statuses move fast in this market — re-verify before travelling to any of these. No agent or seller was contacted. No personal details are included.

## The pick

**Buy first: 424 Spring Ave, Clarksburg** — 2,155 sq ft, city water/sewer, in one of the strongest-value neighbourhoods the scan touched (nearby Spring Ave / Buckhannon Ave houses are valued at $200k–$360k), sold with **extra parcels including a lot with a garage and a 2-bedroom apartment above it** — a built-in house-hack or rental. It needs a heating system and electrical work (budgeted below). One problem: **the price is disputed by my own sources** — the live listing page read $98,500 (116 days, after a $6.4K cut) while the MLS feed and two portal crawls show $139,900. I have costed it at the higher figure: **$139,900 + ~$48K typical repairs ≈ $188K all-in**, against a renovated band of $100–$135/sq ft for Clarksburg (≈$215K+ at the band's low end for this size, before the extra parcels). If the $98,500 reading is the real current price, it is the best buy in this entire scan by a wide margin. Verifying that price is question 1 for Ada in `tasks/from-muse-house-hunt/`.

**Same-weekend second viewing: 106 E Main St, Buckhannon** — the cheapest cost basis in the scan ($75,000 for 2,440 sq ft, $31/sq ft, city utilities, 200-amp service) — held back from the top spot only by listed asbestos siding, one badly damaged room whose cause needs eyes on it, and a main-street frontage.

**The trap this scan caught: 515 Center Ave, Weston** looks like the best brick house in Weston at $95,000 — and its own fine print (Homes.com version of the listing) says it **sustained flood damage in the July 2026 Weston flood, is sold strictly as-is, and some photos show the house before the flood**. Details in its entry below.

## How the scan ran

- **Original brief:** single-family, 1,500+ sq ft, $90,000–$190,000, in/near Fairmont, Clarksburg, Bridgeport, Morgantown (selected pockets), Buckhannon, Weston, Shinnston, Philippi, Grafton. **Expanded at the buyer's request mid-task:** $75,000–$210,000 and 1,400+ sq ft, adding Mannington, Salem, Nutter Fort/Stonewood, Westover, Star City, Kingwood, Elkins and Belington. Entries say which band they came from.
- **Sources:** Realtor.com listing pages opened individually (main source — Homes.com returned "Access Denied" to the browser passes and Zillow presented a human-verification check partway through; Redfin city routing failed for these towns). Facebook Marketplace: the browser could only see search cards (detail pages are login-walled), so Marketplace data here comes from the structured listing records behind the cards, which include beds/baths/sq ft/descriptions where sellers provided them — still no interior photo grading for Marketplace-only listings.
- **Photo grading** is from the listing photos as displayed on 2026-10-05 (16–50 photos per Realtor listing). Where a system or room was not photographed, the entry says so — absence of a photo is not evidence of good condition. One listing's photos are partly **pre-flood** (flagged).
- **Repair budgets** use the typical/low/high figures from `results/fixer-repair-costs` (roof ~$10.5K typical, furnace ~$6.6K, AC ~$5.7K, panel to 200A ~$2.8K, kitchen refresh ~$10K, bath refresh ~$4.5K each, flooring ~$5/sq ft installed, etc.). They are planning estimates from photos and listing text, not quotes — every one of these houses needs an in-person showing and a home inspection before an offer.
- **Renovated bands** (from `fixer-area-land-diy`): Clarksburg ~$100–$135/sq ft, Fairmont ~$146, Buckhannon ~$120–$133, Weston ~$93–$105, Morgantown ~$171–$177, Bridgeport ~$170. No band was established for Grafton, Mannington, Elkins, Kingwood or Belington — $/sq ft there is reported without a band comparison.
- **Standing flags for every house in this scan:** none of the listings displayed a FEMA flood-zone designation, so **check each parcel on the WV Flood Tool before an offer** (Weston and Buckhannon flooded in July 2026; Fairmont in June 2025). All sit in coal counties — mine subsidence cover is include-unless-waived on WV policies (see earlier results). Septic/well is flagged per listing where stated; "not shown" is common.

## Unsure about

- **424 Spring Ave's price** — $98,500 (live detail page, 2026-10-05) vs $139,900 (MLS-derived pages). One of them is stale or wrong; the report costs the house at $139,900 and asks Ada to verify (see `tasks/from-muse-house-hunt/`). Days-on-market readings for several listings also varied between passes (e.g. Center Ave 52 vs 132) — likely relisting artefacts.
- **Flood zones** — no listing displayed a FEMA designation; the flood flags here come from listing language (Center Ave's disclosure, Washington St's flood-insurance offer, Kitson St's and First St's river frontage) and the towns' 2025–2026 flood history, not parcel determinations. Every finalist needs a WV Flood Tool check.
- **Photo limits** — photos are seller marketing: they hide as much as they show (basements, attics, panels and water heaters were rarely photographed; no photo set proves the absence of mould, K&T wiring or foundation movement). 1010 Fay St has only 7 photos; 25 Pinnell St only 6; Marketplace-only listings have no verifiable interior photos at all.
- **Sq ft sources conflict** on several houses (e.g. Spring Ave 2,155 vs 2,262; Redwood St's bed/bath count) — figures used are the listing-page ones, flagged where they conflict.
- **Marketplace data quality** — seller-entered, sometimes duplicated or implausible (noted in Rejected); list dates can be months old even when the card says "Available."
- **Elkins/Kingwood/Belington/Grafton/Mannington** have no renovated band from the earlier research, so their $/sq ft figures carry no value verdict — scores there lean on condition and price alone.
- Repair budgets assume the systems visible or stated; a dead boiler (Spring Ave), a flooded interior (Center Ave) or a torn-up room (E Main St) can each move a budget by five figures after a real inspection.
