## Easiest to win / best fit for a solo developer (rules-based)

1. **PayPal AI Hackathon** — the only one open today, and the most permissive rulebook: any PayPal API plus any AI counts, existing projects are allowed if significantly updated, no team-size cap, and ~20 cash prizes spread across categories give a solo entry many ways to place.
2. **ElevenLabs Chroma Awards** — opens Oct 19 and its sponsor-award structure (24+ sponsor/community prizes on top of division prizes) is generous, but it is a creative film/music/games competition, only last season's rules exist today, and the new season's deadline and prize table are unpublished.
3. **Google (All Things Agentic, closed)** — nothing to enter as of Oct 17, and its last edition was the heaviest lift anyway: new projects only, with a mandatory Gemini 3.5+ model, a Google agent framework, *and* a Google Cloud service in every entry.

## How I checked it

- Every fact above comes from the official event page or the official rules text on the organizer's own platform (all three are Devpost-hosted: Google LLC, ElevenLabs, and PayPal Inc. as sponsors), retrieved 2026-10-17. Deadlines, prize amounts and payout terms are quoted or closely paraphrased from the rules sections (Google §3/§4/§6/§8/§9/§12; ElevenLabs §1/§3/§5/§6/§9; PayPal §1/§3/§4/§6/§7/§8).
- For Google, twelve candidate events were checked on official hosts (Devpost, Kaggle) and rejected as closed or wrong-host before selecting the most recent flagship as comparator. For ElevenLabs, seven prior editions/properties were checked and rejected as closed; the Chroma Awards is the only ElevenLabs-administered property with published Official Rules and a live next season. PayPal's earlier Agent Hackathon and 2025 events were rejected as closed.
- PayPal headline figures were reconciled: the rules' cash prizes sum to $67,500; adding $2,250 of Render credits gives the $69,750 "total prizes" on the event page; the rules control in case of discrepancy (PayPal rules §11.4).
