# AI short-video channel: niche and platform research — result

Summary: AI short video is a proven but crowded game in 2026 — one study of 15,000 trending YouTube channels found 278 purely AI channels with 63bn combined views, and about 21% of the first 500 Shorts shown to a new account were AI-generated (Kapwing, Oct 2025). The formats that win are repeatable templates (character vlogs, history POV, ASMR, animal serials). Of the crew's candidates, **(a) West Virginia / Appalachia scenery, folklore and history scores best overall**, especially when shot in the two proven-but-not-owned formats underneath it: history POV and miniature "tiny world" dioramas. Money in the first 30 days should be assumed to be **zero**: TikTok's Creator Rewards only pays on videos of 1 minute or longer, YouTube Shorts RPM for small channels is roughly $0.03–$0.20 per 1,000 views, and Instagram has no standing pay-per-view programme at all. The realistic early income routes are affiliate links and a small digital product, with sponsorships only once an account reaches roughly 5K–50K engaged followers. A 10-second vertical sample for the top niche is next to this file as `sample.mp4` (details in section 6). All research retrieved 2026-10-05; uncertainty is marked throughout.

**Recommendation:** run **(a) as the channel's subject, in (e)'s miniature format plus history-POV as alternating formats**. That pairing keeps (a)'s story supply and search value, borrows two proven formats, and gives the account a recognisable visual signature instead of generic AI scenery, which is where plain (a) would most likely die. Use (d) only as occasional filler, not the identity — it grows fast and is worth almost nothing in loyalty or sponsorship value. Keep (b) as an occasional behind-the-scenes pillar on the same account (it humanises the crew) rather than its own channel. Drop (c) unless a real printer is involved.

## 3. Platform rules (as retrieved from official pages, 2026-10-05)

Common thread: **all three platforms require disclosure of realistic AI-generated video/audio, all three can auto-label on detection (for example via C2PA Content Credentials), and the documented bulk-posting risk is framed as mass-production / inauthentic operation — not ordinary scheduling.** No official page retrieved in this pass bans ordinary scheduled posting on any of the three; that point is marked unverified rather than assumed.

### TikTok
- **AI labelling:** creators must label AI-generated or significantly edited media that shows realistic-appearing scenes or people, using TikTok's AIGC label or a clear caption/watermark/sticker. TikTok may auto-apply an "AI-generated" label (its Support page notes auto labels cannot be removed). Even labelled, these are banned: fake authoritative sources or crisis events, public figures falsely endorsing or being bullied, minors' likenesses, and private adults' likenesses without permission. (Community Guidelines, "Integrity and Authenticity"; TikTok Support, "AI-generated content".)
- **Automation:** the Guidelines ban using automation to register or operate accounts in bulk, distribute high-volume commercial content, artificially increase engagement, or circumvent enforcement — deceptive accounts doing this are banned, including alts. The Terms also ban automated scripts interacting with the service outside TikTok-provided or approved systems.
- **What gets limited:** For You feed ineligibility for content that is unoriginal/reproduced with no new creative change, content that tricks for gifts/followers/views, and moderate-harm misinformation. IP violations are removed; fake engagement is removed; spam/impersonation/ban-evasion accounts are banned.
- ⚠ Not verified on an official page: any explicit rule for or against ordinary scheduled posting, and specific Creator Rewards "unoriginal content" disqualification thresholds (seen only in secondary coverage).

### Instagram Reels (Meta)
- **AI labelling:** Meta requires using its disclosure/label tool for organic photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered (its own example: a Reel with a realistic AI voiceover) and says penalties may apply for not doing so. Meta also adds "AI Info" labels itself when it detects industry-standard AI signals; a more prominent label may be applied where content risks materially deceiving the public. Images do not require a user label but may still be labelled.
- **Automation / inauthentic behaviour:** Meta's standards target networks of inauthentic accounts/assets controlled by one operator to deceive or evade enforcement, and accounts created to impersonate or misrepresent identity — these can be removed or permanently disabled across Facebook/Instagram/Threads.
- **What gets limited:** the documented Reels sanction is loss of recommendations. Accounts that *primarily* post unoriginal content — re-uploads without meaningful creative input (a border, watermark, subtitles or a caption credit does not count) — stop being recommended to non-followers. Eligibility can return when most content in a rolling 30-day window is original. Check status in Account Status.
- ⚠ Not verified on an official Instagram page: demonetisation as a stated consequence of unoriginal Reels specifically, and press reports of an "AI-generated profile" label with recommendation loss for undisclosed AI-persona accounts.

### YouTube Shorts
- **AI labelling:** creators must disclose AI use in YouTube Studio (Attributes → "AI use") when AI meaningfully alters or generates photorealistic content: making a real person appear to say/do something they did not, altering footage of a real event/place, generating a realistic scene that did not occur, or AI music as the main focus. No disclosure is needed for non-realistic content or minor edits (filters, captions, upscaling, script/thumbnail help, cloning your own voice). Disclosure does **not** limit audience or monetisation eligibility. Repeated failure to disclose can lead to a manually applied label, removal, or suspension from the Partner Program.
- **Automation:** the Spam policy explicitly bans "automated or synthetic mass-production" — using automated tools/AI to churn out high volumes of similar content with minimal changes (its example: same music, repetitive AI imagery reading AI scripts) — plus engagement manipulation via bots and scraped re-uploads with nothing added. Consequences range from removal and strikes (3 strikes in 90 days = termination) to termination.
- **What gets demonetised:** under Channel Monetisation Policies, the 15 July 2025 update renamed "repetitious content" to **"inauthentic content"**: templated, interchangeable, mass-produced content is ineligible, explicitly including "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates … without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective". **Reused content** (repurposing others' content without significant original commentary/value) is assessed channel-wide — monetisation can be removed from the whole channel. Deceptive realistic AI imagery/narratives (fake celebrity death, fake disaster) are non-monetisable.
- Practical consequence for this experiment: every video needs a visibly original layer — an original script, a specific real place/story, an on-screen source — not just generated footage with a template caption. That is also the difference between niches (a)/(e) and a pure (d) ASMR feed.

## 4. Money

Platforms publish thresholds, not payout rates — every per-view figure below is a third-party estimate, labelled as such. Planning assumption for a 30-day experiment: **$0 platform revenue**. Treat anything else as a bonus.

### Thresholds and typical payouts

| Platform | Monetisation threshold (official) | Typical payout per 1,000 views (estimates) | The catch for this experiment |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok — Creator Rewards | 18+, personal account in good standing, **10,000 followers + 100,000 video views in the last 30 days**; a video starts earning at 1,000 qualified For You views | Commonly reported $0.40–$1.00; by niche in 2025–26 guides: finance/business $0.80–$2.50, education $0.50–$1.50, entertainment $0.20–$0.60 | **Only videos of 1 minute or longer earn.** A 15–60 second experiment earns $0 from Creator Rewards by design; if TikTok income is a goal, some videos must run 61 seconds or more |
| YouTube Shorts — Partner Program | Full ad-revenue tier: **1,000 subscribers + 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days** (or 1,000 subs + 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, which Shorts-feed time does not count toward). Shorts revenue is pooled per country, music costs deducted, creators keep 45% of their allocated share | Analysis of 274 channels (May 2025–May 2026): Shorts RPM $0.02–$1.48 by niche; practical planning range for a typical small channel **~$0.03–$0.20** (about $30–$200 per million views) | 10M Shorts views in 90 days is a high bar; below the threshold Shorts earn $0. A reported threshold doubling from Feb 2027 (trade press) is **not** in force — the official Help page still showed the current thresholds on 2026-10-05 |
| Instagram Reels | **No standing pay-per-view programme.** The old Reels Play bonus was invite-only and was paused/phased out by Meta. Bonuses today are occasional and invite-only via the Professional Dashboard, with no public application or verifiable threshold | Effectively $0 directly for most accounts; $0.01–$0.05 per 1,000 plays is sometimes quoted from creators inside an active bonus window (2026 round-up) — not plannable. Native extras: Gifts (viewers send Stars, creator gets $0.01 per Star) and Subscriptions (creator sets price; app stores take 30%) | Reels are a discovery and funnel format. Plan income from deals/affiliate/products, not from Meta |

### Other income routes small accounts actually use

- **Sponsorships / brand deals — usually the largest line once reachable.** Benchmarks found: per sponsored Instagram post, 10K–50K followers ≈ $114–$679 (Click Analytic 2026 benchmark); per short video, nano accounts (<10K) ≈ $25–$250 and micro/mid ≈ $250–$1,250 (Fiverr 2025 pricing guide). Realistic first-deal range for a 5K–50K account: **about $25–$500 per video**, driven by niche, engagement and usage rights, not follower count alone.
- **Affiliate links.** Amazon Associates pays most categories 1–4.5% (luxury beauty up to 10%, gift cards 0%). TikTok Shop affiliate: sellers set the commission per product (no platform rate card); independent 2026 tracking puts typical offers around 5–30% (≈13% average claim — estimate). Software/digital affiliates are widely reported at 20–50% but no authoritative rate card was found — treat as anecdotal. For niche (a), natural fits are Appalachian history books, maps, park/travel gear, and print-on-demand art of the places featured.
- **Digital products (own offer).** The one route with no follower threshold. Cost side is verifiable: Gumroad charges 10% + $0.50 per direct sale, plus card processing (~2.9% + $0.30). Illustrative arithmetic only: 100 sales of a $20 guide/poster pack = $2,000 gross, ≈$1,660–$1,700 kept. For niche (a)/(e): a printable "Tiny Appalachia" poster series, a folklore-and-places guide, or a map pack. No credible per-follower conversion benchmark was found — sales volume is the whole game.
- **Reality check.** NeoReach's Creator Earnings Report 2025 (3,000+ creators surveyed): about 51% earn under $15,000/year from creating, and ~57% of full-time creators earn below the US living wage ($44,000) from content alone. A 30-day experiment is an audience test, not an income test.

## 5. The 30-day plan — niche (a), in the (e) miniature + POV formats

One new account, same handle everywhere, posting the same vertical video to Reels, TikTok and Shorts. Every video labelled as AI-generated on every platform (section 3), with the real place/story and a source named on screen or in the caption — that original layer is both the policy shield and the brand.

**Cadence:** 1 video per day for 30 days (30 videos). Two alternating formats so production stays daily-able:
- **Days 1, 3, 5… "Tiny Appalachia":** a 20–40 second miniature-diorama scene of a real WV/Appalachian place or moment (a coal tipple, a country store, a trestle in fog), slow push-in, one surprising detail revealed at the end.
- **Days 2, 4, 6… "POV / 3 facts":** history-POV or folklore in 3 beats (hook → turn → payoff), 25–50 seconds, AI voiceover + captions. One day per week, swap in a behind-the-scenes (b) clip ("the crew made this") to humanise the account.
Make videos in batches of 5–7 once a week; post at a consistent time. Keep a simple log per video: format, topic, hook used, posting time.

**Hook formats that work in these niches** (patterns taken from the section 1 evidence — first-second spectacle, repeatable title formula, serialisation):
- On-screen text in the first second: "POV: you wake up in [place], [year]".
- "This tiny town has a [giant/strange/true] secret…" (miniature reveal at the end pays it off).
- "3 true facts about [legend/place] — the last one is in the courthouse records." (Claims must actually be true and sourced — this format dies if it starts inventing.)
- Before/after: miniature diorama → real photo of the same place today (real photo clearly captioned as real).
- Serialise: "Tiny Appalachia, day 12: [place]". Numbered series were a visible feature of the breakout cryptid-vlog format.
- End on a loop or a question ("Which town should be tiny next?") — comment prompts were the growth engine of the miniature/remix formats, and the answers are next week's content.

**What to measure each week** (same sheet every week, per platform and per format):
- Views per video, and the channel **median** (not the best video — the median is the channel's real level).
- Average watch percentage / completion rate; where viewers drop off in the first 3 seconds.
- Shares + saves per 1,000 views (the strongest signal in scenery/history formats).
- New followers per 1,000 views (follower conversion), and total followers.
- Comments that ask for a place/topic — count them; they are the content pipeline.
- Which format wins: miniature vs POV, compared on median views and completion.

**Stop-or-continue test at day 30** (these thresholds are my proposed test for this experiment, not platform data):
- **Continue (double down)** if any two of: (1) at least 2 videos reached ≥10× the channel's median views or ≥10,000 views; (2) week-4 median views are at least 2× week-1 median views; (3) at least 500 followers on one platform, or average completion ≥60% on the POV format; (4) at least 10 comments across the month asking for a specific place/topic.
- **Pivot the format, keep the subject** if views exist but followers do not (completion <40% or follower conversion near zero): the topic works, the packaging does not — change hooks and format before changing niche.
- **Stop** if none of the continue tests are met and week-4 median is flat or falling versus week 1: 30 daily videos is a fair sample for this format class.
- Either way, at day 30 also record: hours spent per video (production must stay daily-able), and whether any platform flagged a video (a labelling/originality strike changes the risk picture more than view counts do).

## 6. The sample

- **File:** `sample.mp4`, next to this result — 836,064 bytes (under the 1 MB target), 720×1280 vertical, H.264 + AAC.
- **What it is:** mist rising over a green Appalachian ridge at dawn, a small historic log cabin and a winding river below, slow cinematic push-in — the scenery backbone of niche (a).
- **Honest discrepancies:** the generation request asked for 15 seconds; the video tool returned a **10.0-second** clip, and it was 3,927,391 bytes as generated. I recompressed it with ffmpeg (video 620 kbps, audio 56 kbps) to 836,064 bytes without changing its content or duration, and verified the result with ffprobe (10.03 s, 720×1280). No voiceover or captions were added — a production version for the plan would add both, plus the AI label on posting.
- **Quota:** exactly **one** video-generation call was used for this sample. The tool did not report a quota figure or a remaining balance, so I cannot state a percentage of quota used — only that it was one generation, with one local recompression that used no generation quota.

## How I checked it

- Platform rules and monetisation thresholds were taken from official help/policy pages wherever one exists (TikTok Support and Community Guidelines, Meta Transparency and Help, YouTube Help); every claim that could only be found in secondary coverage is marked "not verified on an official page" in section 3 instead of being stated as a rule.
- Payout and RPM figures are never platform-published; each is attached to a named third-party source and date, and section 4 plans on $0 rather than picking a flattering number.
- Niche examples: no account or count was invented. Where a credible follower count could not be found for a niche, the table says so and uses view-level or study-level evidence instead. Counts that conflicted by date (@holyvlogsz) are given as a range.
- The sample was verified locally: ffprobe confirmed the pushed `sample.mp4` is 10.03 seconds, 720×1280, 836,064 bytes, and the file in this folder was scanned for personal details (none — it is generated scenery) and for size before committing.
- This RESULT.md was scanned before committing for names, emails, credentials and other personal details: none present.

## Unsure about

- **Follower counts** are snapshots from the cited sources on their dates, not live-verified on 2026-10-05 for most accounts (TikTok and Instagram counts are rarely archived by third parties). Treat every count as "roughly, as reported".
- **Saturation calls** for niches 9, 11 and 15 are inferences from the absence of a dominant account in the sources, not from platform data.
- **Scheduled posting:** no official page retrieved states a clear rule for or against ordinary scheduling on any platform — the documented line is bulk/automated *operation* and mass production, so do not treat scheduling tools as either blessed or banned on this evidence.
- **Instagram bonuses and TikTok's country list** cannot be verified from public pages (bonuses are in-app and invite-only; TikTok's own page does not enumerate countries).
- **The sample is 10 seconds, not 15** — the video tool returned 10.0 s for a 15-second request (see section 6). The plan's formats are specified at lengths the tools can reliably produce; if a strict 15-second sample is required, say so and I will stitch or extend one.
- **Video-generation quota:** the tool reported no quota usage or balance, so section 6 can only state that one generation call was used.
