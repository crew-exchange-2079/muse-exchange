# Beauty Sites Market Research — Solo Beauty Professionals in WV / Eastern OH / Eastern KY

Date: 2026-10-11
Scope: West Virginia, eastern Ohio and eastern Kentucky — Huntington, Charleston, Morgantown, Clarksburg/Bridgeport, Parkersburg, Wheeling, Ashland, Portsmouth.
Method: Public info only (public Instagram/Facebook pages, public business listings, official pricing pages). No one was contacted, messaged, followed, or liked. Business pages only, no personal profiles.

## Summary

The niche is real, visible, and badly under-websited. In a 27-business sample across the 8 areas, 17 were booking-page-only, only 4 had a real own-domain website, 1 was Linktree-led, and the rest were social-only with no separate booking/website link found. A $300–$800 one-time price sits inside the proven niche band (see §3). The main competitor is not another freelancer — it is the booking website already included with GlossGenius / Square. A standalone site has to sell what that page does not do: Google search visibility, credibility, and policies/aftercare in one place.

Rough total: **~120–200 visibly active solo/small operators** across the 8 areas (inference, not a census — see §1 count signals).

### Non-spammy outreach norms — synthesis from the rules actually read

1. Professional trade groups almost universally ban cold self-promo ("give more than you take — self-promotion, spam and irrelevant links aren't allowed" recurs in Baddie Aesthetician, LASH WORLD, Ask An Esthetician). Link-dropping and DM solicitation get removed; MLM is banned in several.
2. The sanctioned vendor routes are paid/partnered and explicit: Ask An Esthetician sells admin-approved promo posts ($20 single post); Esthetician Business Network invites brands to discuss sponsorship (https://esthetician.org/).
3. Where self-promo is allowed, it is rationed and conditioned: The Esthetician Forum once a week with location/details; Charleston Small Business Owners 1/week; Huntington Independents ~1/week, introduce yourself first, engage beyond selling; WV Local max 2/day with reciprocity. WV Small Business and the Dayton beauty group are rare explicitly promo-friendly exceptions.
4. What mods consistently allow: value posts (education, troubleshooting, business-growth tips), job/room/class posts in the designated format/cadence, introductions before promotion, and admin-approved/paid partner posts.
5. Highest-leverage channels, in order from the evidence: (a) salon-suite location partnerships (Sola Morgantown + Triadelphia/Wheeling) — one manager, dozens of solo tenants who are by definition rent-paying solo businesses; (b) beauty-school graduate/alumni partnerships — new solos need a first site; (c) small promo-friendly WV groups, posting portfolio work within their 1/week rules, and participating (not pitching) in WV Beauty Industry; (d) paid admin-approved posts in national groups if national reach is ever wanted; (e) Instagram local-tag presence — the WV/city feeds are tiny enough that genuine engagement is visible.

## 5. Honest take: is $300–$800 per site + optional hosting/care worth doing?

Yes, as a focused niche side offer. No, as a generic discounted "websites for anyone" tier — that would undercut a general $1,495 website price and attract the hardest clients.

Why it can work:

- The gap is proven locally: ~65% of the sample has no site Google can rank — a GlossGenius/Square page under someone else's domain, no service pages, no local SEO, policies/FAQs buried in Instagram highlights.
- They already pay for tools: $20–$50/mo for booking software is normal for this niche, so a ~$39–$49/mo care plan is not a new category of spend.
- The payback story is simple: roughly one facial/lash client a month covers the site cost at the service prices shown on their own booking pages.
- Price fit: $300–$800 sits inside the proven niche band (~$300–$1,055 one-time among niche providers in §3), above the Fiverr/Upwork floor ($50–$499), below med-spa agency minimums ($2,500+).

What would make it stand out (and justify the $800 end):

1. Done fast, booking untouched — the site wraps around their existing GlossGenius/Vagaro/Square/Fresha link; no migration, no downtime. Fear of breaking booking is the #1 objection.
2. Google package included — Google Business Profile cleanup plus "esthetician near me / lash lift [city]" service pages and linked reviews. That is what a booking page cannot do, and it is the plausible reason The Skin Study left GlossGenius for its own domain.
3. Beauty-specific pages competitors skip — cancellation policies, aftercare, prep, contraindications/FAQ, and a fast-loading gallery. Solo pros retype these in DMs constantly.
4. Care plan as the real product — $300 alone leaves recurring income on the table. Sweet spot from the comps: ~$549–$799 build + $39–$59/mo care (hosting, edits, Google profile posts), bundling domain + hosting + a monthly edit so cancelling means losing the site. Reserve ~$300 for a true one-pager only.

Risks:

- Sales time per ~$500 deal: trade groups will not let a vendor pitch, so distribution is partnerships + referrals or it is slow.
- Scope creep on photos/copy: require the client to supply photos/text (most already exists on their booking page).
- Churn if care feels optional: see bundling point above.
- WV tax note from prior launch research: website design services are generally taxable in WV — price/quote accordingly.
