# Launching a one-person IT and web side business in West Virginia (2026)

**Task:** `tasks/it-biz-launch-rules/TASK.md` — registration, taxes, Google Business Profile, insurance, contracts, and moonlighting rules for a part-time IT support / small-business website service in north-central WV, from dated public sources. All sources below were retrieved **2026-10-05**. This is research, not legal or tax advice; figures are stated only where a cited source gives them, and gaps are flagged rather than filled in.

**The headline findings, up front:**

- Minimum state cost to launch: **$30 as a sole proprietor, $130 as an LLC** — and the LLC's annual report is only **$25/yr**.
- **WV taxes services by default.** Computer repair, IT support and website design are all taxable services in WV unless a specific exemption applies — the opposite of most states. Budget to collect 6% state sales tax (plus up to 1% municipal) from day one.
- A 2025 law (HB 2451) now exempts very small operators (under $2,500/yr gross, no permanent location in the city) from municipal licences and city B&O tax — but a home-based business *inside* a city may still need that city's licence and owes B&O on gross receipts at roughly 0.7–0.8%.
- Google lets you hide the home address as a service-area business, but verification is basically a live, unedited video now, and sloppy names/addresses are the main suspension triggers.
- Insurance for a solo operator runs roughly **$1,300–$2,500/yr** for general liability + tech E&O; clients who ask for anything usually ask for a certificate showing **$1M** limits.

## 1. Registration: sole proprietor vs single-member LLC in WV

### Sole proprietor

- **Under your own legal name:** there is no formation filing with the Secretary of State. The mandatory state step is the **Business Registration Certificate** (below) — the Tax Division says every individual or business entity must obtain one before engaging in business activity. ([Tax Division — Business Registration](https://tax.wv.gov/Business/BusinessRegistration/Pages/BusinessRegistration.aspx))
- **Under any other name (a DBA):** register a trade name with the WV Secretary of State — **Form TN-1** for sole proprietors/partnerships (LLCs and corporations use Form NR-3). Order matters: you must be tax-registered first; the SOS will not process the trade name until then. **Fee: $0 for sole proprietors/partnerships** ($25 for LLCs/corporations), and a WV trade name **never expires** — it stays in effect until you withdraw it or the business ends. A DBA creates no separate entity and no liability shield. ([Swyft Filings — WV DBA](https://www.swyftfilings.com/dba/west-virginia/); [LegalZoom — WV DBA](https://www.legalzoom.com/articles/file-a-dba-in-west-virginia); [ZenBusiness — WV DBA](https://www.zenbusiness.com/west-virginia-dba/))
- **You need an EIN before registering online:** the One Stop Business Portal requires a federal EIN first, even for a sole proprietor registering there. ([WV One Stop](https://business4.wv.gov/startmybusiness/pages/register-your-business.aspx))

### Single-member LLC

- **Articles of Organization (Form LLD-1)** with the WV Secretary of State: **$100** ($101 online with the $1 portal fee). The SOS may **waive the initial fee for veteran-owned businesses and WV-resident young entrepreneurs (ages 18–29)** — SOS fees only, not the $30 tax registration. ([WV SOS — Register a New WV Business](https://sos.wv.gov/register-new-wv-business))
- **Annual report: $25/yr** ($26 online), filed **January 1 – June 30**; the first is due by June 30 of the year *after* formation. Late filing adds a $50 late fee; continued non-filing risks administrative dissolution. ([BoostSuite — WV LLC cost](https://boostsuite.com/how-to-start-an-llc/cost/west-virginia/))
- **Registered agent — WV is unusual: optional.** The SOS's own form instructions say you *may* maintain an agent of process; if none is named, service defaults to the Secretary of State (W. Va. Code §31B-1-108). What *is* required is a **designated office at a physical WV street address** (no P.O. Box). Many commercial formation sites wrongly claim a WV agent is mandatory — the SOS form language controls. ([SOS LLC form instructions](https://sos.wv.gov/media/289/download?inline=))
- **One Stop flow:** EIN → create a portal account → "Register a Business" → one session files with **three agencies at once**: Secretary of State, Tax Department (issues the Business Registration Certificate) and WorkForce West Virginia (unemployment — relevant only if you hire).
- **Which to pick, in brief:** a sole proprietor is $100 cheaper to start and has no annual report, but offers zero separation between you and the business's debts and liabilities. An LLC costs $100 + $25/yr and, *if* you keep finances genuinely separate, walls the business off from your personal assets — for work that touches clients' networks and data, that separation is usually the point. Tax-wise they start out identical (see §2: a single-member LLC is disregarded and files on Schedule C).

### The WV Business Registration Certificate (both forms need it)

- **Who:** every person or entity doing "business activity" in WV, **including sole proprietors and the self-employed**, before starting; a separate certificate is required **for each location and each business name**.
- **Cost: $30 one-time per location. It is permanent — no renewal** — valid until the legal name, location or ownership changes, or the business closes (then surrender it). Apply via the One Stop Portal or paper Form WV/BUS-APP. ([Tax Division — Registration](http://tax.wv.gov/Business/BusinessRegistration/Pages/BusinessRegistration.aspx); [Tax Division Q&A](https://tax.wv.gov/Business/BusinessRegistration/Pages/BusinessRegistrationQuestionsAndAnswers.aspx))
- **Small-earner exception ⚠️ conflicting figures:** the Tax Division FAQ says you need no certificate at all if you needn't collect/withhold any tax, claim no exemption, **and** had **$4,000 or less** gross business income in the prior tax year. Summaries of 2025's HB 2451 say the threshold rose to **$10,000**. The Tax Division page still shows $4,000 — verify the current number with the Division before relying on either. (An IT business collecting sales tax will generally need the certificate anyway.)

### City business licences (and municipal B&O tax)

Most WV cities require their own licence **and** levy a municipal **Business & Occupation (B&O) tax on gross receipts**, filed quarterly. Two statewide notes first:

- **HB 2451 (2025, effective July 11, 2025):** no city licence may be required of an independent contractor or sole proprietor earning **under $2,500/yr** who has **no permanent physical location in that city**, and businesses under $2,500 gross are exempt from municipal B&O. A home-based business *with* a location in the city may fall outside the licence exemption — read the enrolled text before assuming. ([Enrolled HB 2451](http://www.legis.state.wv.us/bill_status/bills_text.cfm?billdoc=hb2451%20sub%20enr.htm&yr=2025&sesstype=RS&i=2451))
- City licences run on a **July 1 – June 30** year, and your state Business Registration Certificate comes first.

## 2. Taxes

### WV sales tax on your services — yes, by default

WV runs the opposite way from most states. W. Va. Code §11-15-2(18) defines a taxable **"service" as all nonprofessional activities** engaged in for others for a consideration, and the Tax Division's rule (110 CSR 15 §8) applies the **6% consumers sales and service tax** (plus up to 1% municipal tax where adopted, sourced to where the service is performed) to "the furnishing of **all** services, **except** professional services, personal services, [PSC-regulated] services, contracting services, and services rendered by an employee to his employer." The exempt "professional" list is a **closed list of licensed professions** (physicians, lawyers, CPAs, architects, engineers, real-estate brokers…) — **no IT occupation is on it**. The Division's publication TSD-300 (*Sales and Use Tax Exemptions*, revised Feb 2026) states the same exemptions. ([§11-15-2](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/pdf/11-15-2/); [110 CSR 15 §8](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/west-virginia/W-Va-C-S-R-SS-110-15-8); [Municipal sales/use tax](https://tax.wv.gov/business/salesandusetax/municipalsalesandusetax/Pages/MunicipalSalesAndUseTax.aspx))

Applied to the three services in the brief:

- **Computer repair — TAXABLE, parts and labour.** Repair is a nonprofessional service; the regulation's own example says excelling at repairing television sets is "not considered professional." Parts are taxable tangible property, and separately stating parts vs. labour changes nothing — the tax base includes labour, and the rules expressly tax separately stated charges for services "in… maintaining, or repairing" tangible property (110 CSR 15 §129.6). **Tax the whole ticket.** You also owe tax on your own business purchases used in the service activity, except items bought for resale. ([110 CSR 15 §129](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/west-virginia/W-Va-C-S-R-SS-110-15-129))
- **IT support / consulting — TAXABLE by default.** Help-desk, network admin, troubleshooting and general consulting are nonprofessional services for consideration. Three carve-outs matter:
  - **Electronic data processing services are exempt** — §11-15-9h(a)(3). A service that is genuinely data processing (not support or advice) can fall here.
  - **Direct-use exemption** — services directly used in manufacturing, transportation, **communication**, utilities etc. are exempt when sold to businesses in those activities (§11-15-9(b)(2)); the buyer furnishes an exemption certificate/direct-pay permit. Ordinary office IT support does not qualify.
  - Services for **resale** are outside the definition entirely.
- **Website design — TAXABLE by default ⚠️** (no WV publication naming website design was found; the conclusion follows from the statute). Design/build is a nonprofessional service, and to the extent the deliverable is a built site, "sale" expressly includes transfer of **custom software** (§11-15-2(17)), taxed like tangible property. Do not confuse this with the separate exemption for **Internet advertising** (§11-15-9h(a)(5)) — selling ad placement is exempt; designing the site is not. Pure hosting/data-processing elements may fit the EDP exemption — document any split on the invoice.
- **Software:** prewritten software is taxable tangible property **including when delivered electronically**; custom software is likewise taxable in WV; the Tax Division's Streamlined taxability matrix says so for every delivery method. **SaaS ⚠️:** secondary sources treat WV SaaS as taxable, consistent with the default rule, but no Tax Division publication naming SaaS was found — flagged as unverified. ([Taxability matrix](https://tax.wv.gov/Documents/SST/taxabilityMatrix.20090616.pdf))

### Estimated taxes

- **Federal:** profit goes on **Schedule C** (a disregarded single-member LLC files identically), self-employment tax on **Schedule SE — 15.3%** (12.4% Social Security up to the wage base + 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net earnings, applying once net earnings reach $400; half of SE tax is deductible. **Form 1040-ES** estimates are required if you expect to owe **$1,000+**; due **April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15**; safe harbours are 90% of the current year or 100%/110% of the prior year. ([IRS — Estimated taxes](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/estimated-taxes-arent-just-for-the-self-employed); [Schedule SE instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sse))
- **West Virginia (Form WV/IT-140ES):** per the form's instructions, estimated payments are required if you expect to owe **at least $600** in WV tax after withholding; due dates mirror the federal ones; pay via MyTaxes (mytaxes.wvtax.gov) or voucher. Statutory basis §11-21-55. ⚠️ The $600 figure is quoted from the form instructions as reproduced on form mirrors; a live current-year voucher on tax.wv.gov could not be loaded in this pass to re-verify it in place.
- **Side-income wrinkle for a full-time employee:** you can often cover the side income by increasing W-4 withholding at the day job instead of filing quarterly estimates — withholding is treated as paid evenly through the year. (General information; confirm against your own numbers.)

### What to track from day one

Per IRS Publication 583 (*Starting a Business and Keeping Records* — keep income/expense records and supporting documents until the limitations period expires, generally 3 years, 6 for substantial understatement): ([Pub. 583](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p583))

- **All income** — every invoice and payment, whether or not a 1099 ever arrives.
- **Expenses by Schedule C category** — parts/materials (separate resale inventory from shop supplies; it matters for the WV tax treatment above), software subscriptions, tools/equipment with depreciation records, advertising, insurance.
- **Sales tax collected, as its own ledger** — state 6% vs. municipal amounts per invoice, exemption certificates received from exempt business customers, remittance confirmations. Never book collected tax as income.
- **A mileage log** — date, client/purpose, miles. The 2026 IRS standard rate is **72.5¢/mile (Jan 1 – Jun 30) and 76¢/mile (Jul 1 – Dec 31)** after a mid-year increase. ([IRS — Standard mileage rates](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/standard-mileage-rates))
- **1099s both ways** — expect 1099-NECs from business clients and reconcile them to your books. If you pay contractors, collect a **W-9 before first payment**; ⚠️ the 1099 reporting threshold rose from $600 to **$2,000 for payments made after Dec 31, 2025** (OBBBA §70433) per the IRS's updated 1099 instructions — verified via secondary reporting of those instructions rather than read directly on irs.gov in this pass.

## 3. Google Business Profile as a service-area business (no storefront)

Sources for this section are Google's own Business Profile Help pages (retrieved 2026-10-05): [Guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), [Service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481), [Business address](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2853879), [Video verification](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14271705), [Suspended profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145).

- **The model fits — if you serve people in person.** A service-area business (SAB) "visits or delivers to customers directly but doesn't serve customers at their business address." Eligibility requires a physical location customers can visit **or** travelling to customers where they are — there must be in-person contact. A **purely online/virtual-only business is not eligible and cannot be verified**. An IT service that makes house/small-office calls qualifies; a websites-only, never-meet-anyone operation is the risky edge — keep the in-person services (on-site support, setup, training) genuinely part of the offer.
- **One profile for the whole service area.** Set service areas by city, postal code or named area (not radius), **maximum 20 areas**, not extending beyond roughly **2 hours' driving time** from base. Age-restricted categories can't run as SABs (not an issue here). Approved service-area edits can take up to 48 hours to appear.
- **Hiding the home address:** Edit profile → Location → turn **off** "Show business address to customers." The address stays on file with Google (needed for verification) but isn't displayed; the profile shows only the service area. Google's own example for who *should* hide their address is a plumber run from a residential address. Rules around it: only hide it if customers genuinely don't come there; P.O. boxes and remote mailboxes aren't acceptable; a **virtual office is not eligible at all**; a co-working address qualifies only with signage, reception and your own staff present. Showing the address anyway implies permanent signage and that customers are received there — an address/display mismatch is a recognised suspension trigger, and Google reserves the right to decide how the address is displayed.
- **Verification in 2026 — plan on video.** Google chooses the method (video recording, live video call, phone/text, email, postcard exist), and for SABs the documented path is a **video recording**: captured live in the app, **unedited, one continuous take, at least 30 seconds**, no sensitive data or other people's faces. For an SAB it must show three things: **(1) where you operate** — street signs/landmarks near the address (Google explicitly allows neighbourhood landmarks for home-based businesses); **(2) proof the business exists** — professional tools/equipment, workspace, business cards, branded apparel; **(3) proof of management** — performing a service, unlocking a branded work vehicle, or showing a permit/invoice/utility bill matching the profile name. Review can take **up to 5 business days**; rejections come with reasons and you re-record. Core-info edits afterwards can trigger re-verification. ⚠️ "Video is now the dominant method" is industry/SEO-blog reporting — Google doesn't publish method frequencies.
- **Common suspension reasons (all from the guidelines page):** keyword-stuffed business names (the name must match real-world branding — no taglines, service keywords, locations, hours, phone numbers or URLs; Google says this "could result in suspension"); address violations (virtual offices, mailboxes, unstaffed co-working, showing an address where customers aren't received); **duplicate listings** ("only one profile per business"); ineligible business types. Suspension can be profile-level or account-level (a restricted account suspends everything it manages).
- **Reinstatement:** fix the violation *first*, then use the **Business Profile appeals tool** (sign in with the owning account, select the profile, Submit Appeal). Evidence is strongly recommended — **business registration, business licence, tax certificates, utility bills showing the same business name and address as the profile** — and if you open the evidence form you must submit **within 60 minutes**. Do **not** create a new profile for the same business while an appeal is pending. If the account itself is restricted, appeal the account first.

## 4. Insurance

Figures are insurer/industry-published averages for IT consultants/technicians (Insureon, Hiscox, NEXT, MoneyGeek — retrieved 2026-10-05), not quotes. ([Insureon — IT consultant insurance cost](https://www.insureon.com/technology-business-insurance/it-consultants/cost); [MoneyGeek — tech/IT business insurance](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/business/tech-it/software/))

| Coverage | Typical annual cost (solo) |
|---|---|
| General liability ($1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate) | **≈$369/yr** average ($31/mo); tech businesses broadly ~$250–$2,500+/yr by risk |
| Business owner's policy (GL + property bundle) | ≈$638/yr |
| Tech E&O / professional liability (usually bundles third-party cyber) | **≈$899/yr** average at $1M/$1M limits |
| Standalone cyber insurance | ≈$1,540/yr average; small businesses generally ~$400–$8,000+/yr |
| Cross-checks | Hiscox professional liability starts ~$22.50/mo; its worked example (IT consultant, $150K revenue, $500K limit) ≈$515/yr. NEXT typical: GL $25–45/mo, professional $25–45/mo |

- **Budget rule of thumb: ≈$1,300–$2,500/yr** for GL + tech E&O (which usually already includes some cyber); more if you buy standalone cyber at higher limits.
- **What drives the price:** limits and deductible; annual revenue (E&O is priced mainly on revenue); services offered (security work and software development rate higher than general support); how much client personal data you touch (the main cyber driver); security controls — insurers increasingly *require* MFA, backups and endpoint protection to sell cyber at all; claims history; subcontractors.
- **What clients actually ask for:** small-business clients mostly ask for nothing, or simply for proof you're insured. Clients with procurement processes ask for a **certificate of insurance (COI)** before work starts, most commonly **$1M per occurrence for tech E&O and cyber** ($2M+ for regulated data or SLA-backed work). Larger clients add: **additional insured** status on your GL (sometimes E&O/cyber), **primary-and-noncontributory** wording and a **waiver of subrogation** — all added by endorsement through your insurer; a COI alone doesn't change coverage. At the top end (universities, finance, government), requirements jump to $5M cyber with carrier ratings and fidelity bonds — treat those as a signal the job needs a bigger vendor, or price the compliance in.

## 5. Contracts

*General information — templates are a starting point; a WV attorney review before first client use is cheap insurance against a bad clause.*

**A simple IT service agreement should contain:** parties and term; **scope of services and deliverables**, plus an out-of-scope/change-order process; service hours and response-time expectations, with exclusions; **fees, invoicing, late-payment terms and any deposit/retainer**; **data access and credentials** — the client grants access, you handle credentials securely, access is returned/revoked at the end, and who owns backups and data is stated; confidentiality; **limitation of liability** (commonly capped at fees paid over a defined period or a fixed amount, excluding gross negligence/willful misconduct); indemnification; insurance obligations; **termination** (for convenience on notice, for cause, payment for work performed, and data/credential handover on exit); dispute resolution and **governing law (West Virginia)**.

**A website project agreement should contain:** scope/deliverables (pages, features, how many revision rounds, and **client-supplied content deadlines** — the #1 schedule-killer); milestones and acceptance testing; **payment schedule with an up-front deposit (commonly 25–50%)** and final payment due before/at launch; **IP and ownership** — the client owns the final site content and, **on full payment**, the delivered site/design, while you retain rights in your pre-existing code, tools and open-source components (licensed to the client, not transferred); **hosting/maintenance boundaries** — hosting and domain ideally sit in the **client's own accounts**, post-launch support is billed separately or via a care plan, and you give no uptime/security guarantees beyond the host's; a warranty window for bug fixes; termination/kill fee; permission to show the work in your portfolio.

**Template sources (honest free vs. trial labels):**

- **Docracy — genuinely free.** Open-source contract library (freelance service, consulting, independent contractor agreements, NDAs), free to copy and adapt: https://github.com/docracy1/docracy-templates and docracy.com
- **Bonsai — templates free** (account signup; the full platform is a paid subscription). Lawyer-drafted freelance/web-development contracts with e-sign: https://www.hellobonsai.com/contract-template/freelance-creator
- **SCORE — free.** SBA resource partner: free templates and free mentoring — a useful second set of eyes before signing clients: https://www.score.org
- **LawDepot — free trial only** (7 days, then ~$49/mo). Not permanently free.
- **Rocket Lawyer — free trial only** (7 days, then $39.99/mo). Documents made during the trial are yours; ongoing access needs the subscription.

⚠️ Template pricing/trial terms change frequently — verify on the vendor's site before relying on them.

## 6. Moonlighting while employed full-time

*General information only, not legal advice — an employment attorney should review any actual agreement.*

**The norms:**

- **Read your own paper first:** handbook moonlighting/outside-employment policy, offer letter, and anything you signed (non-compete, NDA, invention assignment). Many employers require **advance disclosure or approval** of outside work.
- **Conflict of interest is the red line.** Don't serve your employer's customers, don't compete with the employer, don't divert opportunities that belong to them. The implied **duty of loyalty** generally bars competing with or undercutting your employer *during* employment, even off-hours.
- **Never use employer time, equipment, accounts, software licences, networks or confidential information** for the side business — misuse of company resources is the most common discipline trigger, and employers may lawfully prohibit it.
- Keep day-job performance unimpaired. WV is an **at-will** state and is *not* among the states with a broad statute protecting lawful off-duty activity, so a policy violation can mean discipline up to termination even when the side work itself is legal.
- Keep the two roles visibly separate: your own business name, devices, accounts, contracts and insurance (sections 1, 4, 5).

**WV non-compete basics:**

- **Enforceable, but only if reasonable — and WV has no general statute** either banning or authorising employee non-competes; common law governs. Under *Reddy v. Community Health Foundation of Man* (W. Va. 1982), a covenant is reasonable only if it (1) is no greater than required to protect the employer's legitimate interests (trade secrets, customer goodwill), (2) imposes no undue hardship on the employee, and (3) is not injurious to the public — weighing time, territory and restricted activity. ([LawServer — WV non-competes](https://www.lawserver.com/law/articles/non-competition-agreements-in-west-virginia))
- **WV courts disfavour these covenants and will not rewrite them:** one unreasonable on its face is void rather than judicially narrowed. Overbreadth hurts the employer — but a narrow, well-drafted covenant can be enforced.
- **The only occupation-specific statutory cap is for physicians** — W. Va. Code §47-11E-2 limits physician non-competes to 1 year and 30 road miles. There is **no equivalent cap for IT workers**.
- **There is no federal ban:** the FTC's 2024 non-compete rule was set aside nationwide in *Ryan LLC v. FTC* (Aug 2024) before it took effect, and the FTC dismissed its appeals in September 2025. State law controls; the FTC says it will still police abusive non-competes case-by-case.

**Practical bottom line:** for a WV IT employee, the realistic risks are (a) breaking a written employer policy or a signed *narrow* non-compete/non-solicit, (b) duty-of-loyalty problems if side clients overlap the employer's market, and (c) at-will discipline. The mitigations are disclosure where required, total separation of time/tools/accounts, and never touching the employer's clients or data.
