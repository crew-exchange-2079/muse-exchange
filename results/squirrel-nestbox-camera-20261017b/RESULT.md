## Summary

You can absolutely watch squirrels raise babies from a phone. There are ready-made wooden squirrel houses with a camera built in (solar powered, Wi-Fi, night vision), and there are also good "buy the box and camera separately" combinations. The box itself should follow the University of Missouri Extension design for gray/fox squirrels: a 3-inch entrance hole, a 6×6 to 8×8 inch floor, 15–18 inches tall, mounted 10–30 feet up, facing east or south. Install in autumn (October–November) so squirrels claim it as a winter den before the December–January breeding season and the February–March first litters. Every Wi-Fi camera below works on 2.4 GHz home Wi-Fi (one UK camera is dual-band), and every maker says the same thing about range: there is no fixed distance number — test the Wi-Fi signal at the exact tree before final mounting and add an outdoor extender if it is weak.

## 3. How big should the box be? (eastern gray squirrels)

From the University of Missouri Extension guide G9413, "Nest Boxes for Wildlife" — its gray and fox squirrel row:

- **Entrance hole:** 3 inches in diameter, on the side of the box, positioned toward the tree (2.5 inches from the tree side); the hole sits **10–12 inches above the floor**.
- **Floor:** 6×6 to 8×8 inches. **Box height:** 15–18 inches.
- **Material:** cedar or another rot-resistant wood, or exterior-grade plywood. **Avoid treated lumber and metal boxes.** Don't paint or stain the inside. Use a sloped roof that extends over the sides, with the roof or one side openable for cleaning, and no perch.
- **Drainage:** at least four 3/8-inch holes in the floor.
- **Ventilation:** gaps or 5/8-inch holes, at least two per side, near the top.
- **Bedding:** fill the box about half full with dry leaves — squirrels will rearrange and add their own.

Note how the ready-made products line up: the JCS Nutty Mansion uses dual 3-inch entrances, the KinetCam uses dual 2.5-inch entries with metal chew rings, and ordinary bird boxes (32 mm holes) are far too small — entrance size is the quickest way to tell a real squirrel box from a bird box.

## 4. Placement

- **Height:** 10–30 feet up (MU Extension). The product guidance clusters in the same band: 10–13 ft (KinetCam manual), 10–20 ft (Squirrel Boxes), 12–30 ft (JCS).
- **Tree vs pole:** a tree trunk is the default — pick a healthy tree at least 10 inches in diameter, in or near other trees so squirrels can travel canopy-to-canopy. Pole mounting also works, and MU Extension notes pole-mounted boxes are easier to protect from predators; cone baffles or metal bands help either way.
- **Entrance direction:** face the entrance **east or south** (MU Extension), and away from prevailing winds and direct sun (Squirrel Boxes).
- **Distance from the house / Wi-Fi:** no maker publishes a verified maximum distance in feet — it is simply "as far as your home Wi-Fi reliably reaches the tree." Practical rules from the makers: test the signal at the exact tree before final mounting (KinetCam's manual wants >3 Mbps upload there; JCS requires a strong 2.4 GHz signal), and add an outdoor Wi-Fi extender if it is weak. Almost every camera here is **2.4 GHz**, which carries farther through trees and walls than 5 GHz — that is a point in 2.4 GHz's favour for a tree at the end of a yard. The Blink combo is the exception to think about differently: its camera talks to an indoor Sync Module, so keep the module somewhere with a clean path toward the tree.

## 5. Timing — when to install for spring babies

Gray squirrel breeding and litters (NWCO wildlife-control training materials; Vale of Glamorgan Council grey squirrel guidance; iNaturalist eastern gray squirrel field guide):

- **Breeding:** mainly mid-December through January (a second breeding period follows in May–June).
- **Gestation:** about 40–45 days.
- **First litters:** born **February–March**. Litter size is usually 2–4 (average 3–4). Weaning begins at about 10–12 weeks.
- **Second litters:** June–July (some sources say July–August).

**Best install time for spring babies: autumn — October–November.** Squirrels choose their winter dens in late autumn, and MU Extension advises putting boxes up well before the nesting season because territories are established 3–4 weeks ahead. A box that goes up in October or November can be claimed as a winter den, and the same den is where the February–March babies are most likely to be raised. A box installed in January is not hopeless, but it misses the den-selection window.
