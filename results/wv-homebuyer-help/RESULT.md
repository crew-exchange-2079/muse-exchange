# West Virginia first-time homebuyer help in 2026, in plain terms

**Task:** `tasks/wv-homebuyer-help/TASK.md` — every realistic way for a first-time buyer (single, full-time salaried federal IT contractor) to cut the cash needed and the monthly cost on a $120k–$175k, 1,500+ sq ft fixer-upper in Harrison, Marion, Lewis or Upshur County, buying in roughly 12–24 months. All sources retrieved **2026-10-05**; program rules are paraphrased from the official sources linked — check the links for exact current terms, because WVHDF changes its numbers by memo several times a year. This is research, not financial advice.

**The answer in one paragraph:** The strongest stack in West Virginia is **WVHDF Homeownership + the Low Down Home Loan** — a bond-funded first mortgage posted at **5.79%** (vs. the 7.28% national average the week of Oct 1, 2026) plus up to **$12,000 at 2% for 15 years** toward down payment and closing costs, which on a $150,000 Clarksburg purchase works out to **≈$1,180/month and near-zero cash beyond prepaids** (§4). The catch is a hard **income cap** ($58,000–$72,400 by county) that a salaried IT contractor may well exceed — in which case **Movin' Up + Low Down** (income up to $171,120, but first-mortgage rate posted at 7.50%) or a **USDA Guaranteed** loan (0% down, and — surprisingly — **all seven target towns sit in USDA-eligible territory**) become the main routes. For the fixer-upper itself, the repair money comes from **FHA 203(k)** (Limited up to $75,000 of rehab) or **HomeStyle/CHOICERenovation**, not from WVHDF or USDA Guaranteed, neither of which is a rehab loan.

## 1. WVHDF programs (West Virginia Housing Development Fund)

Sources: [WVHDF programs](https://www.wvhdf.com/programs/single-family-programs-and-resources/), program pages and lender memos linked per item. Framing fact: **Harrison and Marion are "non-targeted" counties; Lewis and Upshur are "targeted"** — that distinction controls the first-time-buyer rule. At $120k–$175k, every purchase-price limit below is non-binding; the limits that bite are income and credit.

### Homeownership Program (the flagship)
- **What:** 30-year fixed, bond-funded; can finance up to 100% of the price when combined with Low Down (below); generally the Fund's lowest rates. The first mortgage itself is delivered as a **conventional (Fannie Mae "HFA Preferred") or government-insured (FHA/VA/USDA) loan** through a participating lender — WVHDF is the programme wrapped around those loan types, not a fourth loan type.
- **First-time rule:** no ownership interest in a principal residence in the **3 years** before closing — **enforced in non-targeted counties (Harrison, Marion)**; repeat buyers are not barred in targeted **Lewis and Upshur**.
- **Income and price limits** (WVHDF chart effective for locks on/after June 4, 2025; income = gross income of everyone on the note/title): **Harrison — income ≤ $67,760, price ≤ $300,000 · Marion — ≤ $72,400 / $300,000 · Lewis — ≤ $61,600 / $300,000 · Upshur — ≤ $58,000 / $300,000.** ([Limits chart (PDF)](https://www.wvhdf.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2025-Income-and-House-Price-Limits-Updated-June-2025-1.pdf)) ⚠️ No newer Homeownership chart was found on wvhdf.com; the Fund's Spring 2026 flyer advertises "increased income limits" but labels them Movin' Up only — confirm at lock.
- **Credit:** the Fund's consumer FAQ gives no hard floor ("low- to mid-600s can generally be acceptable"); its lender Procedural Guide sets the operative overlays for government-insured wraps: **620 minimum representative score, max total DTI 49.99%, automated approval required (no manual downgrades)**; conventional wraps follow Fannie Mae guidelines (secondary sources commonly cite 640 — ⚠️ not stated as a single number on WVHDF's consumer pages). ([Procedural Guide (PDF)](https://www.wvhdf.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Single-Family-Lending-Procedural-Guide-42025.pdf))
- **Rate:** posted on the site and changed by lender memo; as retrieved, **5.790% (APR 6.395%)**, cut from 5.990% on July 31, 2026 after a $60M bond sale. Bond rates move with the Fund's sales — the 12–24-month timeline means today's number is a planning figure, not a promise.
- **Property rules:** WV primary residence on **5 acres or less**; single-family, townhome, approved condo/PUD, doublewide manufactured (no singlewides — existing doublewides built after June 15, 1976 became eligible Jan 5, 2026); no cosigners/non-occupant borrowers; reduced mortgage-insurance coverage and no loan-level price adjustments.

### Movin' Up (and Movin' Up Special) — the fallback when income is too high
- **Differences:** **no first-time-buyer requirement** (repeat buyers welcome statewide), **no acreage limit**, and since **January 5, 2026** the limits are flat statewide: **income ≤ $171,120, price ≤ $350,000**. Accepts FHA/VA/USDA/private MI wraps; the notarised federal-compliance affidavits required for Homeownership are not required here.
- **Rate:** posted **7.500% (APR 8.144%)** at retrieval — roughly **1.7 points above** Homeownership's bond rate. That gap is the real price of exceeding the Homeownership income cap (worth ≈ +$164/month on the §4 example).
- **Movin' Up Special:** for qualifying income ≤80% of area median — reduced rate (posted **7.375%**) and reduced PMI, conventional wraps only. 80% AMI figures (WVHDF quick reference, June 2026): Harrison **$68,160**, Marion **$71,760**, Lewis **$65,280**, Upshur **$60,080**.

### Low Down Home Loan — verified, and the premise needed a correction
- **Current terms (WVHDF's own page + lender memo): up to $12,000 at 2.000% fixed, 15-year fully amortising second mortgage** — repayable, **not forgivable**. It pays for **down payment and/or closing costs** and eligible fees.
- **The history matters:** through most of 2025 the maximum was **$8,000**; the Fund **raised it to $12,000 effective with locks on/after January 22, 2026**. So "$12,000 at 2%" is correct *now* — it's a 2026 improvement, and anyone quoting it from a 2025 article is accidentally right. ([Increase memo (PDF)](https://www.wvhdf.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Low-Down-Home-Loan-Increase_WVHDF_1.21.2026-1.pdf); [Low Down page](https://www.wvhdf.com/programs/single-family-programs-and-resources/low-down-home-loan/))
- **Layering rules:** only behind a **WVHDF first mortgage** (Homeownership or Movin' Up — the consumer page's headline says "Homeownership only," but its own FAQ, the 2026 memos and the Procedural Guide all say both; treat both as operative ⚠️ internal inconsistency flagged). First-lien LTV must be ≥80%; combined LTV ≤105%. **If you refinance later, Low Down must be paid off — WVHDF will not subordinate it.** That is a real constraint on the "buy now, refinance when rates fall" plan.

### Homebuyer education
- **Required for conventional/uninsured wraps** in both programs: Fannie Mae's **HomeView** course, completed by at least one borrower on the note, certificate dated within a year of closing — **free** via the link on WVHDF's Lending Partners site. On government-insured wraps, the agency's own rules govern instead.

### Approved lenders nearby
- WVHDF originates through **60+ partner institutions**; the authoritative roster is its searchable **Find a Lender** database ([wvhdf.com/lenders](https://www.wvhdf.com/lenders/)). Institutions the Fund has publicly named as partners include **WesBanco, MVB Bank (headquartered in Fairmont), United Bank, First Community Bank, City National Bank, First Neighborhood Bank** — MVB, WesBanco, United and First Community all have branches in the Clarksburg/Fairmont/Weston/Buckhannon corridor. ⚠️ The named list comes from a Fund training article and the live directory renders as individual loan-officer profiles; confirm a specific branch's participation in the database or by calling the Fund before relying on it.

## 2. Federal options

### FHA standard (203(b)) — the forgiving baseline
3.5% down at a 580+ score; 500–579 requires 10% down (HUD Handbook 4000.1 minimums; lender overlays common). Upfront MIP **1.75%** (usually financed) + annual MIP **0.55%** at >95% LTV (0.50% at ≤95%) — with <10% down the annual MIP runs **for the life of the loan**. CY2026 loan-limit floor is **$541,287** and all five local counties sit at the floor — limits are irrelevant at this price. No income limit; owner-occupancy required; and FHA minimum property requirements mean a genuine fixer-upper usually needs 203(k), below. ([HUD loan limits](http://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-25-145))

### FHA 203(k) — FHA's rehab loan
One owner-occupied mortgage covering purchase + repairs; licensed-contractor work only (your own labour isn't reimbursable); no luxury items.
- **Limited 203(k):** up to **$75,000** of rehabilitation costs (raised from $35,000 by Mortgagee Letter 2024-13, effective Nov 4, 2024); **non-structural** work only (kitchens/baths, roof, HVAC, plumbing/electrical, flooring, paint, weatherisation, appliances); **no consultant required**; work done within **9 months**; home must stay habitable during the work.
- **Standard 203(k):** minimum **$5,000** of rehab, no program cap beyond the area loan limit; structural work allowed (additions, foundation); a **HUD-approved 203(k) consultant is required**; **12 months** to complete. ([HUD 203(k) page](http://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/single-family-203k))

### Fannie Mae HomeStyle Renovation — the conventional rehab loan
Renovation costs capped at **75% of the lesser of (price + renovation) or the as-completed appraised value** — generous here (a $150k purchase + $60k of work against a $230k as-completed value caps at $157.5k of reno). As low as **3% down** on a 1-unit primary (typical minimum score 620); licensed contractor required, but a **DIY allowance up to 10% of as-completed value** exists on 1-unit owner-occupied homes; up to 6 months of payments can be financed if the home is uninhabitable during work; 15 months to finish. **PMI cancels at 80% LTV** — the structural edge over FHA's life-of-loan MIP at low down payments.

### HomeReady (Fannie) and HomeOne (Freddie) — 3% down without renovation
- **HomeReady:** 3% down; income **≤80% of the property's census-tract AMI** (no cap in designated low-income tracts) — county reference points are the same ≈$58k–$72k band as §1; education required when all borrowers are first-time buyers; reduced MI pricing; repeat buyers allowed.
- **HomeOne:** 3% down; **at least one borrower must be a first-time buyer** (none in the past 3 years); **no income limits and no geographic restrictions** — its distinguishing feature; education required if all borrowers are first-time (Freddie's free CreditSmart Homebuyer U counts); PMI cancelable. For a buyer earning above the WVHDF/HomeReady caps, HomeOne is the cleanest low-down conventional route.
- **CHOICERenovation (Freddie):** HomeStyle's counterpart — same 75%-of-lesser cap architecture; ≥10% contingency reserve (15% if utilities aren't operable); C5/C6-condition homes eligible if the renovation cures the deficiencies (directly relevant to fixer-uppers); can combine with HomeOne up to 97% LTV. The streamlined **CHOICEReno eXPress** covers small projects (≤10% of as-completed value, ≤15% in Duty-to-Serve high-needs rural areas ⚠️ address-specific).

### USDA Rural Development — the 0%-down route, and the towns check out
- **Guaranteed (via lenders):** **0% down**; household adjusted income **≤115% of area median** (published tables show caps around **$103,500 for households of 1–4** in Harrison and Lewis ⚠️ table vintage predates the current fiscal year — confirm in USDA's income tool); **1% upfront guarantee fee (financeable) + 0.35% annual fee for the life of the loan** — cheaper than FHA MIP on both legs; no USDA credit-score floor (most lenders want 640+ for automated approval); primary residence in an eligible area. **Fixer-upper caveat:** the house must meet RD property standards at closing — a standard guaranteed loan is *not* a rehab loan; major defects must be cured by the seller pre-closing or via limited minor-repair escrows.
- **Area eligibility — all seven towns pass at area level:** USDA ineligibility covers only ~0.7% of WV's land (the bigger metros), and ZIP-level boundary data shows **no exclusion zones** for **Clarksburg (26301), Bridgeport (26330 — checked carefully; despite its growth it is not in an exclusion zone), Weston (26452), Buckhannon (26201 — 91 of 92 sampled listings eligible), Shinnston (26431), Philippi (26416 — 56 of 56 sampled)**; **Fairmont (26554)** is eligible across most of the area with the noisiest data — **address-level verification is essential there**, and prudent everywhere, via the official lookup at **eligibility.sc.egov.usda.gov** before writing an offer. (⚠️ Town findings rest on a third-party mirror of USDA boundary data; the official interactive map could not be read at street level in this pass.)
- **Direct (via USDA RD offices):** for **low-income (≤80% AMI) and very-low-income (≤50% AMI)** households who can't get credit elsewhere on reasonable terms: typically no down payment, **payment assistance** that can bring the effective rate as low as **1%** (the subsidy is **recaptured** on sale/transfer/move-out), terms to **33 years (38 for very-low-income)** — and uniquely, a direct loan **can purchase and repair** an existing house ("must be in good repair or placed in good repair with loan funds"). County area loan limits apply; funding is appropriations-limited and processing is slow — apply early at the local RD office. A single salaried contractor's income may exceed the direct caps; the Guaranteed programme is the realistic USDA route for this brief.

### VA
Requires an eligible veteran, service member or qualifying surviving spouse with a Certificate of Eligibility. A civilian contractor does not qualify — **VA is not applicable here.**

## 3. Local and employer-side help

- **HomeOwnership Center, Inc. (Elkins)** — the sleeper find for **Lewis and Upshur** counties: a NeighborWorks nonprofit lender whose **Down Payment & Closing Cost Assistance Program lends up to $12,500** (score ≤699, DTI <41%) **or up to $20,000** (score 700+), accessed via referral from a participating local bank; its 8-county area expressly includes Lewis and Upshur. It also provides the pre-purchase counseling and homebuyer education the other programmes want. ([hocwv.org](https://hocwv.org/info-on-hocs-down-payment-assistance-program/))
- **Fairmont-Morgantown Housing Authority (Marion County)** — advertises a **down-payment assistance programme** (CDBG-funded; the public application states no fixed dollar cap ⚠️) plus homebuyer education and free pre-purchase counseling. (Its separate Morgantown Homecoming $5,000 forgivable programme is WVU-employee-only and out of scope.) ([fmhousing.com](http://www.fmhousing.com/CDBG_APPLICATION_NEW.pdf))
- **Harrison County / Clarksburg:** ⚠️ **no standalone city or county purchase-DPA with published amounts was found.** Local housing-authority money is rehab-oriented (e.g., FHLBank AHP-funded rehabilitation of owner-occupied homes in Clarksburg's Monticello neighbourhood via the Clarksburg-Harrison Regional Housing Authority). The practical Harrison route is WVHDF + local lenders + counseling (below).
- **Habitat for Humanity:** **Mon Valley HFH** serves **Marion** County (standard model: need + ability to pay + sweat equity; income roughly 50–60% of AMI; pre-applications invited). **Buckhannon River HFH** serves **Upshur** (still active — it partnered in the July 2026 flood-relief drive; no published application window). ⚠️ **Habitat for Humanity of Harrison County shows "Permanently Closed" in current directory listings** — treat as not a live route; **Lewis County has no affiliate** (its nonprofit route is HOC, above). Note also: despite the name, "Mountaineer Habitat" serves Kanawha/Putnam, not this region. Realistic read: Habitat's income bands (~50–60% AMI) sit below most salaried buyers, and timelines are years — a footnote, not a plan.
- **Credit unions:** **Fairmont Federal Credit Union** (branches incl. Bridgeport, Shinnston) lists a First Time Homebuyer Program (education/program-level; no published DPA amount ⚠️). **Home FCU** (Clarksburg) is the community-chartered CU for Harrison County residents, with mortgage lending and buyer guidance but no specific DPA offer found. **West Virginia Central FCU** (Parkersburg-based) explicitly packages **WVHDF first-time-buyer products with down-payment and closing-cost assistance** — the state stack delivered through a credit union.
- **HUD-approved counseling in-region:** HomeOwnership Center (Lewis/Upshur), Fairmont-Morgantown Housing Authority (Marion), and Consumer Credit Counseling Service of North Central WV (Clarksburg, Harrison — ⚠️ directory entry is dated; confirm current HUD status). Free or cheap, and the certificates double as programme education requirements.
- **Employer-side (general):** do not budget for it. Employer homeownership assistance is genuinely rare — a June 2026 survey of 1,000 full-time workers found **~1% of employers offer homeownership-assistance programmes**; where help exists at large federal contractors it is overwhelmingly **relocation assistance tied to a company-directed move**, not a benefit for buying near your existing job. There is **no federal programme for contractor employees as such** — and direct federal employees have no general DPA either (their housing support is PCS/relocation benefits for directed transfers). Ask HR once, in writing, whether any home-buying benefit or an EAP-linked mortgage discount exists — then plan as if the answer is no.
- **WV tax extras:** ⚠️ **WV has no first-time homebuyer savings account and no first-time-buyer tax credit.** A First Time Home Buyer Savings Account Act (HB 3375, 2025 — up to $5k/yr individual tax subtraction, $50k cap) was introduced and referred to House Finance in March 2025 and **did not pass** (a 2023 twin died the same way). Do not let any plan depend on it; watch the 2027 session if the timeline is 24 months.

## 4. Cost math for a $150,000 purchase — the best 3 combinations

**Shared assumptions (all sourced or flagged):** price $150,000 in **Clarksburg** (Harrison County); taxes **≈$1,370/yr ($114/mo)** — computed from the 2024 Class II levy (total ≈152.2¢ per $100 on a $90,000 assessment = 60% of market; outside city limits it drops to ≈$1,032/yr) ⚠️ latest pinnable levy is tax-year 2024; insurance **$1,200/yr ($100/mo)** — interpolated from WV averages (ValuePenguin: $200k of dwelling coverage averages $1,361/yr) ⚠️ no published $150k-tier figure; closing costs (lender/title/etc.) modelled at **2.5% = $3,750** within the CFPB's 2–5% range — WV's deed excise tax (≈$845–$1,175 total on this price in Harrison County) is **seller-paid by statute** and excluded; prepaids/escrow set-up ≈ **$2,000**. Market-rate loans use Freddie Mac's PMMS for the week of Oct 1, 2026: **30-yr fixed 7.28%** (excellent-credit, 20%-down basis — a low-down borrower may price higher ⚠️). WVHDF rates are as posted at retrieval (Homeownership **5.79%**; Movin' Up **7.50%**). Repairs are **excluded** from all three (see the renovation loans in §2 — a 203(k)/HomeStyle wrap changes the loan amount, not the structure of the comparison).

| | **1. WVHDF Homeownership + Low Down** | **2. FHA 3.5% down** | **3. USDA Guaranteed** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Down payment | $4,500 (3%) | $5,250 (3.5%) | **$0** |
| First-mortgage amount | $145,500 @ **5.79%** | $144,750 + 1.75% upfront MIP financed = $147,283 @ 7.28% | $150,000 + 1% guarantee fee financed = $151,500 @ 7.28% |
| Assistance | **Low Down $12,000 @ 2%, 15 yrs = $77/mo** | — | — |
| Principal & interest | **$853/mo** | $1,008/mo | $1,037/mo |
| Mortgage insurance | PMI ≈$36/mo (reduced WVHDF coverage — ⚠️ estimate at ~0.30%/yr; cancels at 80% LTV) | MIP $66/mo, **life of loan** | Annual fee $44/mo, life of loan |
| Taxes + insurance escrow | $214/mo | $214/mo | $214/mo |
| **Total monthly (PITI + MI + 2nd)** | **≈$1,181** | **≈$1,288** | **≈$1,294** |
| Cash needed at closing | Down + closing + prepaids ≈ $10,250, **minus $12,000 Low Down → ≈$0–2,000 out of pocket** (depends on final prepaid/escrow tally) | ≈ **$11,000** | ≈ **$5,750** (closing + prepaids only) |

**Reading the table:**
- **Combination 1 wins on both axes** — the bond rate (≈1.5 points under market) is worth ~$155/month against FHA, and Low Down reduces the cash hurdle to the prepaid/escrow remainder. Its gates are the §1 **income cap** and the 3-year first-time rule. **If income exceeds the county cap**, the same stack on **Movin' Up (7.50%)** prices at ≈**$1,345/mo** (P&I $1,017) — still low-cash, no longer low-monthly; at that point compare hard against Combination 3 and a plain HomeOne.
- **Combination 2's** hidden cost is permanence: MIP never drops at 3.5% down, so the exit is a refinance — and refinancing is also the only exit from a Low Down second (it must be paid off, not subordinated) and from USDA's annual fee. Life-of-loan insurance is the toll every low-down route charges in some currency.
- **Combination 3** needs the **address** to be USDA-eligible (likely in all seven towns, §2) and **household income ≤115% AMI**; it is also the only one of the three that can't tolerate a house needing real work before closing.
- Reference point: a conventional 20%-down loan at the same PMMS rate totals ≈$1,035/mo — the "cost of low-down" across the three combos is roughly **$145–$260/month**, bought with $20k–$30k of cash not spent. Over a 3–5-year hold that's a trade most first-time buyers should take, especially with the WVHDF rate.

## 5. The 12-month prep checklist

**Credit (months 1–12)**
- Pull your free reports (AnnualCreditReport.com) now; dispute errors in writing — fixes take 30–90 days, which is why this starts a year out.
- Targets: **620** is the WVHDF government-wrap floor, **640+** unlocks USDA automated approval and better conventional pricing, **680–740** is where PMI pricing improves sharply. Pay revolving balances below ~30% (then ~10%) of limits; no new credit lines or car loans in the final 6 months; keep old cards open.
- Know which score counts: WVHDF uses the *representative* (middle) score; every programme counts **all** income of everyone on the note against its cap — model the cap against your gross early (§1), because it chooses your programme.

**Savings (months 1–12)**
- Even with a near-zero-cash stack (§4), budget closing-day surprises + moving + the first repair wave: a sane target is **$6,000–$10,000 liquid** beyond any down payment, in a high-yield savings account, with a paper trail (lenders source every large deposit — no mattress money, no unexplained transfers).
- Separately seed the **repair fund**: the fixer-upper plan phases cosmetic work from cash (see `results/fixer-repair-costs/RESULT.md`), but roof/HVAC-class failures don't phase — hold the first $5k of repair reserve before you shop.

**Documents (assemble once, keep updated)**
- 2 years of tax returns + W-2s, 30 days of pay stubs, 2–3 months of bank/investment statements, photo ID, and — contractor-specific — a verification-of-employment your employer's HR will actually complete quickly (contract transitions and option-year gaps are the classic federal-contractor underwriting hiccup; a letter confirming position, salary and contract status pre-empts it).

**Education & counseling (months 2–6)**
- Take the **free** Fannie Mae **HomeView** course via WVHDF's link (required for conventional wraps; certificate valid 1 year — time it inside 12 months of closing).
- Book one session with the HUD-approved counselor for your county (§3) — free, and it doubles as an eligibility pre-check for HOC/FMHA assistance.

**Programme positioning (months 6–10)**
- Run your exact address shortlist through the **USDA property-eligibility lookup** and your income through the **USDA income tool**; run the same income against the **WVHDF county caps** — the two answers pick your lane (§4).
- Interview **two WVHDF participating lenders** (§1) and one USDA-approved lender; ask each for a written estimate on the *same* house price so the stacks compare cleanly.

**Pre-approval & shopping (months 10–12)**
- Get a full **pre-approval** (not pre-qualification) ~60–90 days before serious shopping — it expires, and sellers in the $120–175k band take pre-approved offers more seriously; refresh documents rather than restarting.
- For fixer-uppers, get the lender's **203(k)/HomeStyle desk** involved *before* the offer: renovation loans need contractor bids and, for Standard 203(k), a HUD consultant — the timeline is weeks longer than a plain loan, and your offer should carry the longer closing window.
- Lock strategy: WVHDF rates change by bond-sale memo, not daily market moves — your lender watches the Fund's rate sheet; market loans follow the PMMS trend.

**Red flags — programme fine print that bites later**
- **Owner-occupancy:** every programme here requires the home to be your **primary residence** (FHA/USDA/WVHDF typically within 60 days of closing, maintained ~1 year minimum). Renting it out early can trigger due-on-sale/acceleration and, for USDA Direct, **subsidy recapture**.
- **Recapture, two kinds:** USDA Direct payment assistance is recaptured on sale/transfer/move-out; and WVHDF Homeownership loans made from tax-exempt bonds can carry a **federal recapture tax** if you sell within 9 years at a gain with substantially higher income — most sellers owe little or none, but know it exists before choosing Homeownership for a short hold. ⚠️ Recapture-tax detail is standard programme disclosure; confirm the current notice wording with the lender.
- **Low Down is a loan, not a grant:** $77/mo for 15 years, second lien, **must be paid off on refinance** (no subordination) — it slightly reduces future refi flexibility.
- **Resale/condition limits:** USDA Guaranteed and FHA require the house to meet property standards at closing — a cheap house that can't pass appraisal kills the loan, not just the deal; renovation wraps (203(k)/HomeStyle/CHOICE) exist precisely for those, with their own contractor and timeline rules. WVHDF's **5-acre cap** (Homeownership) and modest-house expectations also quietly disqualify some rural fixer-uppers — check acreage early.
- **Timeline risk:** USDA Direct and some assistance programmes are funding-dependent and slow; in a 12–24-month plan, file those applications months ahead and keep a WVHDF/FHA fallback warm.

## How I checked it

Four parallel research passes (WVHDF programmes; federal programmes + USDA town eligibility; local/employer help; cost inputs), each instructed to prefer official sources (wvhdf.com, HUD, USDA RD, the GSEs, WV Tax Division, Freddie Mac's PMMS release), to verify the specific numbers the brief named, and to flag anything unverifiable rather than estimate it. Headline verifications that changed the answer: the Low Down maximum was $8,000 through most of 2025 and only became $12,000 on January 22, 2026; Movin' Up's limits were flattened statewide on January 5, 2026; Bridgeport — assumed ineligible for USDA — shows no exclusion zone in boundary data. The §4 figures were computed from first principles (standard amortisation) out of the sourced rates, levy, insurance and fee inputs, with every input's source or ⚠️ stated beside it. Earlier results (`fixer-repair-costs`, `fixer-listings-scan`) were used for repair-cost context only, not re-derived.

2. **WVHDF Low Down eligibility wording** — the Fund's consumer page headline says "Homeownership only" while its FAQ, 2026 lender memos and Procedural Guide say Homeownership *and* Movin' Up; this report follows the memos/guide.
3. **WVHDF conventional credit floor** — no single figure on consumer pages (620 is the documented government-wrap overlay; 640 is secondary-source convention).
