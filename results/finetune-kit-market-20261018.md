# Small-model fine-tuning (LoRA/QLoRA) kit and service market — 2026-11-17

**Scope:** who sells small-model fine-tuning as a packaged kit or done-for-you service to individuals and small businesses, and at what prices. Read date for every fact below: **2026-11-17**. All sources are public web pages; no login, account, purchase, message or comment was used. Prices are as listed on the source pages on that date; Udemy prices in particular are sale prices and vary by account, region and date.

**Headline finding:** the market splits into four price worlds that barely touch each other. Self-serve learning (courses and template kits) clusters at $0–$19. Hosted per-token fine-tuning costs about $4–$15 for a realistic small job. A flat-rate done-for-you QLoRA service exists at $5–$39. And agency done-for-you projects start at $600 and run to $300,000+. The hole in the middle — a low-price, self-serve kit that takes a non-technical buyer from raw business data to a working GGUF model on their own 24 GB GPU, with proof the tune worked — is not filled by any offer found.

## 3. Gaps: what buyers in public threads ask for that no listed offer covers

Each gap is tied to a thread; the thread links are the evidence. "No listed offer covers" means none of the 27 offers in section 1.

**Gap 1 — Turning raw business material into training data.** Buyers do not have clean example sets and cannot write them. FinetuneDB's Show HN post states it plainly: "creating and managing high-quality datasets is a real bottleneck when fine-tuning LLMs" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41789176). Kiln's Show HN post says the same from the product side: "products simply don't have datasets, and datasets can't keep up with product evolution" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42383284). In the HN thread "Most efficient way to fine-tune an LLM in 2024?", a commenter notes the first step is to "list some question and answer examples" and that "it's amazing how many people are unwilling or unable to do this" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39934480). No kit or course in section 1 ships a dataset-building tool — only teaching about datasets, or (finetuna, agencies) a demand that the buyer supply a clean JSONL first. The $9 Gumroad flow (#2) is the closest: it converts spreadsheet rows to JSONL, but the buyer must still write every example.

**Gap 2 — A no-code path for a non-programmer with private data.** "Ask HN: Is there a ChatGPT 'in a box' for private data?" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34170115) is a buyer asking for a turn-key private-data solution; the top answer says fine-tuning private data "requires programming skills and some knowledge of machine learning" and points non-programmers at turn-key platforms. No low-price kit in section 1 is built for a non-programmer: the kits assume spreadsheets or code, the courses assume Python, and the only no-code tool mentioned in the wild (LLaMA-Factory's Web UI, described at https://www.aitoolspro.tech/blog/how-to-fine-tune-small-llm-on-your-own-data-free) is free open-source software with no packaged, supported kit around it.

**Gap 3 — Proof the tune worked, on the buyer's own task.** Buyers ask how to tell whether a fine-tune helped at all. FinetuneDB's pitch includes "evaluation workflows that allow non-technical contributors to annotate data, review model outputs, and refine responses" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41789176) — i.e. eval is a product feature, not a given. In the HN efficiency thread, the advice is to "evaluate if you want the marginal gains" after a QLoRA run (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39934480). Among cheap offers, only finetuna (#18) ships any before/after evidence (validation loss + 20 held-out prompts answered side by side), and it is a report on a service run, not a reusable eval harness the buyer owns. No kit or course ships a ready-made eval set with pass/fail thresholds for the buyer's own task.

**Gap 4 — What hardware do I actually need? (The 24 GB question.)** "Ask HN: What local machines are people using to train LLMs?" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39028370) is full of buyers confused about what fits where; one commenter notes "with qlora, 7b models could be fitted in 24 GB VRAM" while others quote multi-GPU setups for the same job. No offer in section 1 publishes a tested VRAM/sizing table (model x sequence length x method on a single 24 GB card) — buyers must piece it together from guides such as https://www.local-llm.net/guides/fine-tuning-local-model/, which gives rough VRAM bands but no tested 24 GB matrix.

**Gap 5 — Getting the finished model into daily use (GGUF/Ollama, structured output).** In the HN efficiency thread, a buyer describes the goal concretely: "a 2 GB file GGUF quantised model I could run on any device with a specific JSON grammar" built with PEFT/QLoRA (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39934480). Guides describe the merge-and-convert steps (e.g. https://www.local-llm.net/guides/fine-tuning-local-model/), but no cheap kit ships a one-command train-to-GGUF-to-Ollama path with a Modelfile and a JSON grammar. finetuna (#18) is the only offer that delivers GGUF files directly — and it does the training for you rather than teaching you to do it. The one course covering the loop (#10) teaches it but provides no runnable export script.

**Gap 6 — Your data never leaves your machine.** The "ChatGPT in a box for private data" thread (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34170115) is driven by privacy: the buyer's data cannot go to a vendor. Every hosted API (#15–17) requires uploading the dataset; finetuna's default training runs on community RunPod hosts and its own FAQ says "no personal data there", with privacy costing +50% for a datacenter GPU (https://finetuna.io/). No low-price kit is built around fully local training on the buyer's own GPU with an explicit "nothing leaves this machine" guarantee.

**Gap 7 — The second pass: preference data (DPO) and keeping the model fresh.** The HN efficiency thread advises: "After you fine tune it with 100m token dataset, use DPO to polish it off. You need to create a DPO dataset for that" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39934480). Kiln's post adds the maintenance angle: datasets "can't keep up with product evolution" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42383284). OneShot's Show HN post (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46263508) describes the same need from the other side — routing failed outputs to humans to build corrected training data — and notes niche industries "require a ton of examples, fine-tuning and re-prompting". No offer in section 1 covers DPO dataset creation, re-training cadence, or drift checks; hosted APIs sell DPO as a 2–2.5x price multiplier with no help building the preference data.

## 4. Five positioning ideas for a new low-price kit (single 24 GB consumer GPU)

Each idea is tied to a gap from section 3. Suggested price anchors use the section 2 medians: kits median $9, courses median $19, cheapest DFY $5–$39, agency entry $600.

**Idea 1 — "Dataset-in-a-box" (Gap 1).** A kit whose core product is not the training script but the dataset builder: a spreadsheet template with example-quality rules, a converter (spreadsheet/CSV/tickets/docs to clean JSONL), a "write 50 seed examples, then bootstrap 500 with a teacher model" workflow, and a bad-example detector (duplicates, mixed formats, leaky answers). Sell the outcome "200 reviewed examples in an afternoon". Price $29–$39: above the $7–$9 Gumroad kits (which assume data exists) and below the $30 Fiverr floor, and it attacks the cost agencies charge thousands for.

**Idea 2 — "Private by default" local kit (Gap 6, Gap 2).** Everything runs on the buyer's own 24 GB card (RTX 4090/3090 class); the sales line is "your data never leaves this machine — no upload, no account, no API key". Ship a single tested script + a plain-English setup guide a non-programmer can follow, with a pre-flight check that verifies VRAM, drivers and disk before anything trains. Price $29: the privacy guarantee is the differentiator against the $5–$39 finetuna service and the per-token APIs, which all require uploading data.

**Idea 3 — "Proof it worked" eval pack (Gap 3).** Bundle a reusable eval harness with the kit: the buyer writes 30–50 test prompts with expected answers, the kit runs base vs tuned side by side, scores format/accuracy, and prints a pass/fail verdict with a "do not ship" threshold. Include a one-page report template the buyer can show a boss or client. Price $19–$29 as a standalone add-on to any training kit, or $39 bundled with Idea 1 — it is the only cheap product that answers "did the tune actually help?" with the buyer's own numbers instead of a loss curve.

**Idea 4 — "GGUF in one command" (Gap 5).** A kit whose deliverable is a runnable local model, not a lesson: one command that trains, merges, quantises to q4_k_m, writes the Ollama Modelfile with the buyer's system prompt, and ships a JSON-output grammar for structured answers. Include the "2 GB model on any device" recipe from the HN thread and a test script that proves the model loads in Ollama/LM Studio before the buyer is done. Price $19–$29: it competes with the $19 Udemy course that only teaches this loop (#10) by actually doing it, and undercuts the $5–$39 service while leaving the buyer self-sufficient.

**Idea 5 — "24 GB sizing card + DPO second pass + refresh plan" (Gaps 4 and 7).** A kit built around a tested table — which models (1B–14B) fit on a single 24 GB card at which sequence lengths, with LoRA vs QLoRA, and how long each takes — plus a DPO preference-data template (good/bad answer pairs) and a 90-day refresh checklist (when to re-train, what drift looks like, how to add new examples without breaking the old behaviour). Price $24–$34. No competitor publishes the sizing table, and nobody at any price sells the DPO-dataset template or the maintenance plan to individuals; this is the kit for the buyer who already did one fine-tune and wants the second one to be better.

**Positioning summary:** the crowded price points are $0–$19 (learn it yourself) and $600+ (pay an agency). The open space is $19–$39 for a kit that does the three things nobody cheap does: build the dataset, prove the result, and deliver a runnable local model — on the buyer's own 24 GB GPU, with their data never leaving the machine.
