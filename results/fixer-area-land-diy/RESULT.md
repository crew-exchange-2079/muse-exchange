# Fixer-upper: resale, neighbourhoods, land and DIY — north-central WV

**Task:** `tasks/fixer-area-land-diy/TASK.md` — follow-up to `results/fixer-upper-research` and `results/fixer-repair-costs`. Same buyer profile: a 1,500+ sq ft fixer-upper in Harrison, Marion, Monongalia, Lewis or Upshur County, WV, to live in, and maybe rent or sell in 3–5 years. All figures below were retrieved from public sources on 2026-10-05; links are given per claim. Portal numbers in these small markets rest on thin samples (often 15–90 sales) and different portals disagree — ranges and conflicts are shown, not smoothed over. No personal details are included.

## Short summary

- **No source publishes a renovated-vs-unrenovated price split** for these towns; §1 builds a labelled proxy instead. The pattern it shows: renovated product sells around **$100–$135/sq ft in Clarksburg, ~$146 in Fairmont, ~$120–$133 in Buckhannon, ~$93–$105 in Weston** — and **~$170–$177 in Bridgeport and Morgantown**, which are effectively different markets. In the cheap towns the renovated ceiling is low enough that a mid-range renovation can easily cost more than the value it adds.
- **Flood is the region's live risk, not a theoretical one:** Weston and Buckhannon flooded badly in **July 2026**, Fairmont in **June 2025**. Check every parcel on the WV Flood Tool, not just the FEMA map.
- Schools (SchoolDigger 2025): Monongalia 5th of 55 districts, Marion 9th, Harrison 19th (with Bridgeport HS a standout), Upshur 43rd, Lewis 52nd.
- Broadband is good inside every town (gigabit cable/fibre common) and falls off quickly outside town limits.
- **Land:** assume mineral severance is possible until a *mineral* title search says otherwise; check mine maps (pre-1970 maps are incomplete); septic needs a county-health perc and a 10,000 sq ft reserve area; a new state law gives an ADU by right **inside municipalities only**.
- **DIY:** WV lets owners work on their own home without a contractor licence, and even do their own electrical — but **HVAC always needs a certified technician, septic needs a DHHR-certified installer, and wells need a certified driller**. Permits are a patchwork: Upshur County requires one in unincorporated areas, Harrison requires a Location Improvement Permit, Monongalia County has no county building-code enforcement at all.
- **Renting later:** FY2025 HUD 3-bed FMRs run **$1,144 (Lewis) to $1,283 (Monongalia)**. WV deposits have **no cap** but must be returned in 60 days with itemisation; wrongful withholding costs the deposit plus 1.5× damages. Room-by-room renting is a real market only in **Morgantown** (WVU).
- **Running costs:** a $150,000 house carries roughly **$980–$1,245/yr** property tax (worked example, §6); old wiring/panels and old roofs can block insurance entirely — the WV FAIR Plan (last resort) caps dwellings at $200,000 and offers no liability cover. Gas heat is by far the cheapest fuel where available; oil and propane are the expensive legacy fuels.

### How to estimate ARV on a specific house, and the over-improvement ceiling

1. Pull **renovated comparable sales** — same town/neighbourhood, similar size, actually renovated, sold in the last 6–12 months. In thin markets there may only be 3–5; use all of them and widen the radius before widening the time window.
2. ARV ≈ renovated-comp $/sq ft × your sq ft, adjusted for bed/bath count and lot. Cross-check against the town's renovated band above.
3. Sanity-check the deal: **purchase + renovation + holding/closing costs should stay under ~80–85% of ARV** if a 3–5 year exit matters (looser than a flipper's 70% rule because the buyer also gets the use of the house — but the discipline is the same).
4. **The ceiling is the renovated band, and it is low in the cheap towns.** In Clarksburg a full mid-range renovation at, say, $60–$80/sq ft all-in (see `fixer-repair-costs`) on a house bought at $90/sq ft lands at $150–$170/sq ft of cost against a $100–$135/sq ft resale band — money in that does not come back at resale; it only pays as somewhere nicer to live. In Weston the same logic is tighter still. In Bridgeport and Morgantown the ceiling is high enough that quality renovation can pencil — but purchase prices there already price much of it in.
5. Appraisals follow comps, not receipts: in a street of unrenovated houses, a renovated house appraises toward the street, which caps both resale and any refinance.

## 2. Neighbourhoods

### Flood-zone exposure

- **Weston & Buckhannon — July 21, 2026:** severe flash flooding; 5–7 in. of rain around Buckhannon, 4.5 in. in 37 minutes at one gauge. Lewis and Upshur were among the counties eligible for FEMA assistance (application deadline extended to **Oct 17, 2026**). The West Fork River at Weston reportedly crested ~18.8 ft (single narrative source — treat as approximate); Stonewall Jackson Dam (completed 1988, built for West Fork flood control) held back much of the flow and the town still flooded.
- **Fairmont — June 14–15, 2025 ("Father's Day flood"):** 3+ in. of rain in 75 minutes, flash flooding, water rescues, a partial apartment-building collapse on Locust Ave (NWS Pittsburgh event page).
- **Clarksburg:** sits on Elk Creek, a tributary of the West Fork River; the West Fork joins the Tygart Valley River at Fairmont to form the Monongahela. The benchmark storm is **November 1985** (6–7 in. in 24 hours across Weston/Clarksburg/Fairmont; West Fork at Weston crested 24.10 ft, 7+ ft above flood stage).
- **Morgantown:** FEMA's Flood Insurance Study names Nov 5, 1985 as the greatest known Monongahela flood there; Deckers Creek floods repeatedly, and Monongahela backwater worsens flooding on lower Pompano Run and Dents Run. Storm flooding has also damaged **Westover homes outside the mapped floodplain** whose owners had no flood insurance — mapped-zone status understates risk here.
- **How to check a specific lot:** the **WV Flood Tool** (WV GIS Technical Center) gives parcel-level zone, advisory flood heights and a link to the official FEMA panel; FEMA's Flood Map Service Center is the regulatory source (high-risk = ≥1% annual chance, i.e. at least 1-in-4 over a 30-year mortgage). Then walk the lot after rain (§3).

### Schools (SchoolDigger 2025 district rankings, of 55 WV districts — one aggregator, indicative)

- **Monongalia — 5th, 5 stars.** Morgantown HS and University HS both in the statewide top 10.
- **Marion — 9th, 4 stars** (92.5% graduation rate noted).
- **Harrison — 19th,** sharply split: **Bridgeport HS is 5-star and among WV's top high schools**; the district's other high schools lag state averages — school assignment matters street by street.
- **Upshur — 43rd, 1 star** at district level, though with a 92% graduation rate (top 20% in WV per Public School Review).
- **Lewis — 52nd of 55, 0 stars;** middle/high school scores well below state averages, a couple of elementary schools above average.

### Broadband

- **In town, choice is real:** Bridgeport lists ~12 ISPs (Frontier Fiber headline up to 7 Gbps, Xfinity 2 Gbps, Spectrum and Citynet fibre ~1 Gbps; Citynet reaches ~88% of Bridgeport homes per one aggregator). Clarksburg ~10 ISPs with Frontier Fiber; Morgantown ~10 (Kinetic Fiber, Frontier Fiber).
- **Weston:** 10 providers covering ~99% of the city; Frontier Fiber to ~95% of addresses, Shentel cable to ~94% (FCC data as of Dec 31, 2024, via aggregator).
- **Buckhannon:** 10 providers covering ~99% of the city; Frontier Fiber ~80%, Optimum cable ~91%, Micrologic fixed wireless ~96% at ~50 Mbps (FCC data as of Dec 31, 2025, via aggregator).
- **Fairmont:** no town-level page was retrieved — a gap (see Unsure about); expect the same pattern as its peer towns.
- **Pattern:** gigabit cable/fibre is common inside town limits; rural addresses fall back to DSL, fixed wireless or satellite. A state-backed build-out (Armstrong, 500+ miles of fibre, BEAD/LEAD/E-ACAM funding, 5,000+ previously unserved locations) is closing some rural gaps. Verify any specific address on the FCC National Broadband Map before relying on it for work-from-home.

### Commute — one I-79 spine

All five towns sit on or near I-79 (exits are mile-based):

- **Weston:** directly on I-79 — exits 91, 96, 99.
- **Buckhannon:** the only one **not on I-79** — ≈12 miles east of exit 99 via US 33 (derived from route-log milepoints).
- **Clarksburg:** exits 115–119. **Bridgeport:** exits 121–125 (exit 124 is signed for United Hospital Center and the airport).
- **Fairmont:** exits 132–139.
- **Morgantown:** exits 146–155, and the **I-79/I-68 junction** — the only town with a second interstate.
- Inter-town distances (estimates derived from exit spacing): Weston→Clarksburg ≈ 20 interstate miles; Clarksburg/Bridgeport→Fairmont ≈ 13–18; Fairmont→Morgantown ≈ 15. Practically: the whole corridor is one labour market — a job anywhere from Weston to Morgantown is commutable from any of these towns.

### Crime trend

No town-level trend data was retrieved (see Unsure about). Statewide, FBI figures as summarised by a secondary aggregator put WV violent crime at 363.9 per 100k in 2024 (384.6 in 2015; volatile in between, 398.2 in 2022) and property crime at 1,769 per 100k in 2024, down from 2,495.3 in 2015 — a long-run decline in property crime, a flatter violent-crime trend. ### Planned growth

- **Bridgeport — WVU Medicine / United Hospital Center:** a **$48M, 48,000 sq ft outpatient surgery centre** (8 ORs), part of >$460M in WVU Medicine capital projects announced Apr 2025, completion anticipated **Dec 2026**.
- **Morgantown — WVU Medicine flagship:** >$1B in construction under way system-wide; Ruby Memorial's new eye institute (~3× current size) nearing completion, vacated space earmarked for a future cancer hospital in the early 2030s.
- **I-79 corridor (WVDOH):** >$180M of projects from Harrison County to the PA line — the Marion County section widened to three lanes each way (~$73M, completed Oct 2024), $62.4M of bridge renovations Clarksburg→PA line (to May 2027), $45.5M of bridge replacements between Lost Creek and I-68, a proposed Harmony Grove interchange for the Morgantown Industrial Park, and the **Exit 155 Chaplin Hill** diverging-diamond rebuild in Morgantown ($54.3M federal grant + $67.2M state + $10M local).
- **Population direction 2020→2026 (estimates):** Monongalia **+2.0%** (5th-fastest-growing WV county), Upshur +0.2%, Marion −1.4%, Harrison −2.8%, Lewis −4.2%. Growth signals concentrate in Morgantown and Bridgeport; no Fairmont-, Weston- or Buckhannon-specific major projects were retrieved.

### Ranked shortlist — value plus resale

1. **Bridgeport** — best schools in the set, hospital growth, premium resale band (~$170/sq ft). The catch: the $100k–$175k fixer budget is below its median, so stock is scarce and competitive.
2. **Morgantown (carefully chosen pockets)** — deepest buyer pool, the only real room-rental market, strongest growth; but fixers start ~$140–$160/sq ft and flood pockets (Deckers Creek, Westover) need parcel-level checks.
3. **Fairmont** — the value pick on the corridor: renovated band ~$146/sq ft against fixer entries ~$90–$120, good district ranking, I-79 in town. Asking prices are running ahead of closings — negotiate against sold comps, and respect the June 2025 flood footprint.
4. **Buckhannon** — pleasant college town, renovated band $120–$133 vs entries $85–$110; downsides are the 12-mile hop to I-79, the July 2026 flood, and thin sales data.
5. **Clarksburg (selected streets)** — cheapest entry and improving slowly, but the $100–$135 renovated ceiling punishes over-renovation; buy only where renovated comps already exist nearby.
6. **Weston** — cheapest of all and directly on I-79, but the lowest resale ceiling (~$93–$105), the weakest school ranking, the steepest population decline and a fresh flood history. Works as a cheap place to live; weakest as a 3–5 year resale bet.

## 3. Land — what to check on a WV lot

### Mineral rights: severed or not

- WV recognises a **separate mineral estate** (coal, oil, gas, other minerals) that can be severed from the surface by deed, reservation or old lease. Severance deeds from the coal/timber/early oil-and-gas era are common in title chains; a seller may own "surface only." No reliable statewide percentage severed was found — do not quote one; it is parcel-specific.
- WV Code ch. 55 art. 12A deals specifically with severed and unknown/non-claiming mineral owners, including leasing by a special commissioner; severance is not undone by surface possession alone (§55-2-1a).
- **How to check, in order:** (1) the **county clerk's record room** for that county — deed books, grantor-grantee indexes, oil & gas lease books, right-of-way books; trace the chain back well before the coal/oil boom for reservations ("excepting and reserving all coal, oil and gas"); the WVU WV LEAP guide walks through assessor records → map/parcel → deed book/page → record-room indexes, and warns an informal search is not reliable — a certified title opinion may be needed. (2) Commission a **mineral title search**, not just a standard surface title search — ask the WV attorney/title company/landman in writing whether minerals, coal, oil & gas, timber and existing leases were searched, and list the exceptions in the title commitment. (3) Check *activity*, not just ownership: recorded leases, assignments, pooling language, surface-use agreements. (4) Search wells near the parcel in the **WVDEP Oil & Gas** viewer (operator-reported data; accuracy not guaranteed).
- **Why it matters:** a mineral owner/lessee can hold an implied right to reasonable surface use to extract, subject to the severance deed and WV surface-owner law. Get the deed language reviewed **before** closing.

### Mine subsidence

- Check the **WVGES Mine Map Archives** (~47,000 underground mine maps, many scanned online; coverage fairly good since ~1970, **many pre-1970 maps missing — "no map shown" ≠ "no mine"**), the WVGES Coal Bed Mapping mined-out areas, and the **WVDEP TAGIS** viewers (Mining Data Browser, AML data).
- The **WVDEP Office of Abandoned Mine Lands & Reclamation** handles pre-1977 abandoned mines, subsidence, mine fires, highwalls, open portals and mining-impacted water.
- Insurance: in these coal counties mine subsidence cover is include-unless-waived (WV Code §33-30-6, via BRIM) with a $200,000 per-structure cap; confirm the current cap, deductible and the 30-day effect lag at quote time (mechanics in `results/fixer-repair-costs`).
- **Red flags:** a mapped mined-out seam at shallow depth under the lot, stepped foundation cracks, doors out of square in a pattern, sudden yard depressions, mine mouths/air shafts, coal refuse, orange mine drainage in ditches.

### Slope and drainage

- Before buying or building, do a **soil/site review** (WVU Extension guidance): soil maps, flood frequency, septic limitations; talk to NRCS / WVU Extension. Wet soils, shallow rock or a high water table limit both septic and foundations.
- Walk the lot **after rain** if possible: standing water, swales crossing the lot, washed-out driveway, crushed/undersized culverts, retaining walls leaning or built without drainage, fill placed on a slope or bench (also a septic-denial reason), water staining in basement/crawlspace, neighbours' drainage directed onto the lot.
- **Typical fix costs** (national planning ranges from Angi/HomeYou-type guides — no WV price list exists; steep, wet or undermined sites price above them; get a local excavator's quote, and a geotechnical opinion if there is active sliding):
  - French drain **$20–$50/linear ft** (typical job $2,000–$7,000); trench drain $30–$70/ft; swale $5–$15/ft ($800–$3,000); dry well $300–$1,000 each.
  - Retaining walls **$12.50–$80/sq ft installed** (average project ~$6,100); walls over ~4 ft typically need engineering and drainage behind the wall.
  - Driveway culvert **$800–$8,000** depending on pipe size/material and frontage.
  - Gravel driveway **$600–$3,500** for a short driveway (average ~$1,800); long, steep access roads, switchbacks and stream crossings cost far more. A new entrance on a state route needs a **WV Division of Highways encroachment permit** and a DOH-spec culvert.

### Well and septic vs public water and sewer

- **First verify public service actually reaches the lot** — with the city utility or Public Service District serving that address, not the seller: main location, extension distance, who pays for extension (usually the applicant), tap fee (in the utility's PSC tariff; retrieved examples run $300–$750 for sewer taps) and whether sewer is gravity or grinder-pump. Get extension costs in writing before closing.
- **Septic process:** permitting runs through the **county health department sanitarian** under WV onsite-sewage rules. The site evaluation/perc (state form SS-182A) records perc rate, and a 6-ft hole check for water or solid rock. Procedure targets: sewage applications reviewed within 21 business days. **Common denial reasons:** no 10,000 sq ft septic reserve area staked, soil too shallow over seasonal high water or bedrock, reserve area in fill/benched/flood-prone ground, setbacks to streams, cuts, wells or property lines not met, sinkholes/karst. Installers must be DHHR-certified (see §4). *No single statewide perc fee was verified* — fees are set locally; call the specific county health department for the site-evaluation, permit and re-inspection fees (test-pit digging is usually the applicant's cost).
- **Wells:** a health-department **permit is required before drilling, modifying or abandoning** a well, and the well must be drilled by a **WV-certified well driller**. For an existing house, ask for the well log/completion report, the septic permit and as-built, and any prior denial letters — and have the septic pumped/inspected and the well water tested (coliform, nitrates, metals) during the inspection contingency. **A failed perc on a vacant lot can make it unbuildable for a conventional system** — never close on land before the perc result.

### Zoning for a garage or accessory unit

- WV Code §8A-7-1: a municipality **or** a county may adopt zoning; municipal zoning covers the whole town, county zoning may cover only parts of the county. Many unincorporated areas have **no zoning at all**; some counties zone only specific districts.
- **In a town/city:** expect zoning plus building permits — setbacks, lot coverage, height, accessory-use conditions, floodplain permit where applicable, historic-district rules where applicable.
- **New municipal ADU law (WV Code art. 8-42):** a municipality **shall not prohibit at least one ADU by right** on a lot with a single-family dwelling (attached, detached or internal conversion). Detached/attached ADUs are capped at **75% of the house's floor area or 1,000 sq ft, whichever is less**; no extra parking, family-relationship or street-improvement conditions may be imposed; ADU impact fees are capped at **$250**. Historic-district design rules are excepted. **This right is municipal only** — it does not by itself create an ADU right in an unincorporated county area.
- **In unincorporated areas** (where no zoning applies), a garage/ADU is governed instead by: a building permit *if* the county has one (§4), the county floodplain ordinance (near-universal), **health-department septic capacity for the added bedrooms/flow**, subdivision covenants/HOA rules, and recorded plat setbacks. Worked example: in Monongalia County's West Run Zoning District a land-use permit is required for outbuildings such as garages (fences, landscaping, retaining walls and in-kind replacements need none).
- Checklist either way: zoning verification letter, septic reserve/capacity, floodplain determination, driveway permit, utility tap capacity for a second unit, and whether short-term rental of an ADU is separately regulated locally (not verified statewide).

### Rights-of-way and gas lines

- Recorded easements/ROWs (utility, pipeline, road, access, drainage) appear as **exceptions in the title commitment** and as documents in the county clerk's records (§3, mineral rights). Ask the title company to **plot them on the survey** — a legal description alone hides where a ROW crosses a building site.
- Unrecorded/prescriptive access, old farm lanes, shared drives and utility lines in place can still bind a buyer. Require a current boundary survey showing visible easements, encroachments and **legal access to a public road** — landlocked/access-by-permission is a closing-stopper.
- Read the easement language: width, purpose (one pipeline vs "pipelines"), surface restoration, building/planting prohibitions over the line, exclusivity.
- **Pipeline checks:** the **PHMSA NPMS Public Map Viewer** shows gas *transmission* and hazardous-liquid pipelines by county — but **NPMS does not contain gathering or distribution lines** (the field gathering lines common in WV shale areas, or the service line to a house). A blank NPMS map proves nothing: gathering/local lines surface in clerk-recorded ROWs, the title commitment, WVDEP well records, ground markers and a line-locate — and always call 811 before digging.

## 4. DIY vs hire

### The WV legal frame

- **Contractor licensing:** the WV Contractor Licensing Board (under the **Division of Labor**), WV Code ch. 30 art. 42. A contractor's licence is required when total project cost (labour + materials) is **≥ $5,000 residential / ≥ $25,000 commercial** (threshold raised by HB 2006, effective June 15, 2021; painting and landscaping are exempt from licensing). **Anyone you hire above the threshold should hold a Division of Labor licence in the right classification — verify it.**
- **Homeowner exemption:** the Act exempts **work personally performed on a structure by the owner or occupant** — an owner doing their own work on their own home needs no contractor licence, regardless of project value.
- **Permits are a patchwork.** WV has a State Building Code (administered via the State Fire Commission), but **local jurisdictions choose whether to adopt and enforce it**, and no other code may be enforced. So:
  - **Incorporated towns/cities** (Clarksburg, Bridgeport, Fairmont, Morgantown, Buckhannon, Weston…) run their own permit/code offices (e.g. Morgantown's online Access Portal also handles rental registration).
  - **Monongalia County (unincorporated):** the County Planning Commission states there is **no local enforcement of building code** at county level. County requirements are narrower: a land-use permit in the West Run Zoning District (maintenance, re-roofing, re-siding, window/door replacement and interior-only work need none), a **floodplain development permit** ($20 application) for floodplain work, DOH encroachment permits, and a Real Property Improvement Form filed with the Assessor.
  - **Harrison County (unincorporated):** a **Location Improvement Permit** from the Planning Department (residential $100; additions/remodels under $2,500 $10; normal maintenance $25; demolition $50; floodplain residential $300). The county has also adopted the **International Property Maintenance Code** for unincorporated areas.
  - **Upshur County (unincorporated):** a **Building Permit Ordinance covers all unincorporated areas** — a permit is required *before* any site preparation, development, renovation, improvement, construction or relocation of any building (including manufactured homes); floodplain work must also comply with the county Floodplain Ordinance.
  - **Marion and Lewis counties:** no general unincorporated residential building-permit requirement was found (not proof none exists — ask the county commission). Everywhere, the near-universal county-level requirements are **floodplain permits** and **health-department permits** (septic/well), whatever the building-code position.
  - Where a jurisdiction has adopted the code, electrical permits/inspections run through that office; in non-adopting areas there may be no county electrical inspection for a single-family home — but **utilities typically require an inspection/sign-off before (re)connecting service**, and floodplain certificates can gate utility connection.

### Jobs that legally need licensed/certified trades

- **HVAC — always hire.** All HVAC work in WV requires an **HVAC Technician Certification regardless of dollar amount** (Division of Labor); a contractor licence with HVAC classification is additionally required at $5,000+ residential. Furnaces, heat pumps, refrigerant work: not DIY at any budget.
- **Septic installation — hire.** Installers must be **DHHR-certified (Class I/II)**, and a Division of Labor contractor's licence is also necessary to obtain a permit to install an onsite system (64 CSR 9). DIY septic installation is effectively not an option.
- **Well drilling — hire.** Health-department permit first; drilling only by a **WV-certified well driller** (64 CSR 19). Narrow exception: an owner installing a *pump* on a new private well on their own property need not be a certified pump installer but must arrange to sit the Pump Installer Exam (≥70% to pass).
- **Electrical — legally DIY-able for owners, practically split.** Electricians are licensed by the **State Fire Marshal** (WV Code §29-3B), but §29-3B-3 / 103 CSR 5 exempt a person doing electrical work **on property they own or lease** (or their immediate family's). Local permit/inspection rules still apply where adopted, utilities control meter/service reconnections, and insurers/lenders may expect licensed work. **Panel and service-entrance work is the classic hire-it-out job even where owner work is exempt.**
- **Plumbing/gas:** plumbers are certified by the Division of Labor (Master/Journeyman/In-Training, §21-14). No separate WV gas-fitter licence was found — gas work sits under plumbing/HVAC certification plus local permits; treat gas lines as hire-it-out on safety grounds regardless.

### What an owner with a full-time job can realistically do

Labour is roughly 40–60% of most renovation invoices (see `fixer-repair-costs`), so the "saving" column is the labour share avoided; times assume evenings/weekends and a first-timer's pace of roughly 2–3× a pro's. Skill: ● basic, ●● moderate, ●●● advanced.

| Job | Skill | Realistic DIY time | Money saved vs hiring (labour share) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Demolition, cleanout, dump runs | ● | 2–5 weekends | Most of a $2k–$5k line | Dust control and a dumpster are the real costs |

**Rule of thumb for a full-time worker:** one room at a time, cosmetics and prep yourself, and spend the hiring budget exactly where the law or the risk demands it — HVAC, septic, well, service/panel electrical, gas, structural and roof.

### WV landlord rules in brief (WV Code ch. 37)

- **Security deposits (art. 6A):** **no statutory cap** on the amount; no interest or separate-account requirement. Return deadline: **within 60 days** after the tenancy ends, **or 45 days after a new tenant takes occupancy, whichever is shorter**, with a **written itemisation** of deductions (rent/late fees owed, damage beyond reasonable wear and tear, unpaid tenant utilities, removal/storage of tenant property). If damage exceeds the deposit and a contractor is needed, notice within the period buys 15 extra days to itemise. Deduction records must be kept ≥1 year and produced within 72 hours on request. **Wrongful withholding: the tenant can recover the amount withheld plus damages of 1.5× that amount.**
- **Ending a tenancy (§37-6-5):** year-to-year needs **written notice ≥3 months** before the year's end; periodic (e.g. month-to-month) needs **one full rental period's** notice; no notice is required for a fixed-term lease ending on its stated date, and the lease can set its own notice rule.
- **Eviction (§55-3A):** no self-help — removal goes through magistrate or circuit court on a "Petition for Summary Relief — Wrongful Occupation" (grounds: rent arrears, lease breach, deliberate/negligent damage). The process is fast: hearing set **5–10 judicial days** after filing, the tenant's written defence due within 5 days of notice, physical removal by the **sheriff** under a writ of possession. WV requires **no pre-suit notice/cure period** for nonpayment or breach (terminating a periodic tenancy under §37-6-5 is a separate step).
- **Habitability (§37-6-30):** the landlord must deliver and keep the unit **fit and habitable**, comply with applicable health/safety/fire/housing codes (unless the failure is the tenant's fault), keep common areas clean, safe and in repair, and maintain supplied electrical/plumbing/heating in good working order — Legal Aid of WV's outline adds running water, hot water and reasonable heat **Oct 1–Apr 30**.

### House hacking — is it common?

- **Morgantown: yes, institutionalised.** WVU runs an Off-Campus Housing office because most students live off campus; student houses near the Downtown/Evansdale/Health Sciences campuses are routinely rented **by the bedroom**, and "parent buys a house, student lives in it with 1–2 housemates whose rent covers the mortgage" is an actively marketed local strategy. Cautions: the **City of Morgantown requires rental registration**, and city occupancy/zoning rules limit unrelated occupants in single-family zones — check the specific address before assuming a 4–5 bed student let is legal.
- **Everywhere else: thin and informal.** No sourced evidence of an organised room-rental market was found in Harrison, Marion, Lewis or Upshur (Buckhannon's WV Wesleyan College is a small demand source, but no data was retrieved). Whole-house renting to families and local workers is the norm. A rented room in a lived-in house is still a tenancy under the same ch. 37 rules (deposits, habitability, §55-3A eviction) — no separate "lodger" fast-track was found in WV law.

## 6. Running costs of an old house

### Insurance problems: old wiring, old roofs

- **Knob-and-tube wiring** (common pre-1950, ungrounded, insulation deteriorates): many carriers **will not write new policies at all** on homes with live knob-and-tube; others require a full electrical inspection, surcharge, or limit cover until it is upgraded. **Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels** and old fuse boxes are flagged as high fire risk, and replacement may be required before a policy binds. Galvanised or polybutylene plumbing can likewise block binding until replaced or certified functional.
- **Roof age:** some insurers will not issue/renew on roofs **20+ years old** without an inspection, and older roofs are often settled at **actual cash value** (depreciated) rather than replacement cost — a major exposure if a storm hits in year one.
- **WV last resort — the FAIR Plan** (WV Essential Property Insurance Association, via the Offices of the Insurance Commissioner): for owners "unable to secure insurance in the voluntary market." Terms that matter: dwelling cover capped at **$200,000**, **no liability cover**, application through a licensed agent after trying the voluntary market, and an inspection that determines insurability and rates. Its underwriting standards require property to be reasonably maintained with **wiring in good condition**, and it can decline/restrict for loose/broken/exposed wiring, roof/wall/plumbing leaks, or **electric service under 60 amps** — i.e. even the last-resort plan gates on exactly the old-house defects above. (The FAIR Plan also bundles mine subsidence cover up to $200,000 unless waived.)
- No source publishes WV premium amounts by defect type — the effects are directional: inspection, surcharge, ACV settlement, non-renewal, or last-resort placement. **Practical step: get insurance quotes during the inspection contingency, with the wiring type, panel brand and roof age disclosed — an uninsurable-at-a-sane-price house is a reason to renegotiate or walk.**

### WV property tax

- **The 60% rule:** WV Code §11-3-1(a) — property is assessed annually as of **July 1 at 60% of its true and actual value** (the price in a voluntary sale).
- **Classes:** Class II is property "owned, used and occupied by the owner exclusively for residential purposes" (plus qualifying farms). Rates are quoted in cents per $100 of assessed value, and **the Class II rate is exactly half the Class III/IV rate**. Statutory maxima for Class II: State 0.50¢, County 28.6¢, School 45.9¢ — plus voter-approved excess levies on top (county excess levies need 60% voter approval).
- **Reassessment after sale:** values follow the county assessor's **three-year cycle** — under §11-1C every parcel is visited in repeating 3-year cycles under a county valuation plan. **A sale does not reset the assessment to the sale price** (no sale-chasing provision). A cheap fixer purchase does not lock in a low assessment either — the cycle catches up to market value on its own schedule, and a major renovation is exactly the kind of change a cycle visit notices.
- **Homestead Exemption (§11-6B-3):** the first **$20,000 of assessed value** is exempt for an owner-occupier who is **65 or older, or certified permanently and totally disabled**, a WV resident for the **two consecutive calendar years** before the tax year, claiming no similar exemption in another state, and occupying the home on July 1. One per homestead; ends on transfer unless the new owner qualifies. (Bills to raise it to $25k–$40k from 2027 were introduced in 2025 but **not enacted** as found.)
- **Worked example — $150,000 house:** assessed value $150,000 × 60% = **$90,000**. Using the closest official levy table found (**Taylor County, tax year 2026** — adjacent north-central county; no Harrison/Marion table was retrieved), Class II district totals run 1.0899% of assessed value (rural) to 1.3831% (in-town), giving **≈$981/yr rural to ≈$1,245/yr in town**; with the Homestead Exemption, **≈$763–$968/yr**. Cross-check: Census-based medians are $872 (Harrison) and $989 (Marion) — consistent, since many homes there are appraised below $150,000.

### Heating fuel options and costs

- **Fuel mix (EIA RECS 2020, WV):** natural gas supplies ~56% of residential site energy, electricity ~39%, propane ~3% (fuel-oil cell suppressed; 2020 survey data, shares of energy not households). In practice: gas furnace/boiler where mains exist; heat pump, electric resistance, propane or oil off-main.
- **Prices (EIA):** WV residential natural gas **$13.80/Mcf in 2025** ($15.26 in 2024, $15.61 in 2023 — most recent full-year series, released Aug 2026). WV residential electricity **16.19¢/kWh** (Electric Power Monthly, most recent month retrieved). EIA does **not** survey WV for propane/heating oil; nearest official figures (week of Mar 30, 2026, end of the 2025–26 season): propane U.S. average $2.674/gal (East Coast residential higher, ~$3.24–$3.60/gal in EIA snapshots republished by NPGA), heating oil U.S. average $4.711/gal, Lower Atlantic $4.532/gal. ⚠ EIA's Sep 2026 STEO reportedly forecasts 2026 U.S. residential heating oil averaging ~$5.10/gal vs $3.62 in 2025 — the March figures are likely a floor.
- **Cost ordering** (EIA Winter Fuels Outlook 2025–26 projected national winter household spend: **gas $642 < electricity $1,133 < propane $1,210 < oil $1,390**; my own $/MMBtu arithmetic on the prices above gives the same order: gas ≈$13/MMBtu input ≪ heat pump ≈$19 at COP ~2.5 < propane ≈$29 ≲ oil ≈$33 input < electric resistance ≈$47). **For an old WV house: gas heat is by far the cheapest where the main reaches; a heat pump is the best off-main option; propane and oil are the expensive legacy fuels; straight electric resistance is the costliest of all.** An old house with oil heat and a tired boiler is a strong candidate for a heat-pump conversion budget line (installed by a certified HVAC tech, §4).
