# WV Fixer-Upper Research Brief — North-Central West Virginia

North-Central West Virginia • Harrison · Marion · Barbour • October 2026

# Good bones, ugly face: a fixer-upper brief
For a first-time buyer targeting 1,500+ sq ft, ideally 3 bed / 2 bath, at $100,000–$175,000 in Clarksburg, Bridgeport, Lost Creek, Stonewood, Fairmont, White Hall and Philippi — with cosmetic work phased from monthly cash over 1–3 years, then keep, rent, or sell.
Mortgage context: ~7.2–7.3% (supplied)
Costs are national proxies — no WV price book found
Rents are listing medians, thin samples
**Bottom line:** The price target is realistic in Clarksburg, Fairmont (older stock), Philippi and the smaller towns — and **below the median in Bridgeport**, the premium outlier. The deal only works if the bones pass. Any two major bones failures together (roof + foundation, panel + rewire, septic + HVAC) typically exceed the equity cushion at this price point.
Phased cash is often cleaner than a renovation loan for a truly cosmetic house. Renovation loans add consultants, licensed-contractor-only rules, escrow draws and 45–90-day closings. Use one only if health/safety or lender-required repairs must be financed, or the house won't qualify for a plain mortgage as-is.
Highest resale return is exterior + minor, not major: garage door, steel entry door, stone veneer, siding, and a *minor* kitchen remodel — Cost vs Value 2025. A major kitchen is the classic over-improvement in a $150k–$250k resale market.

## 1 Fixer-upper scorecard — bones vs face
Take this per listing. **Bones** = walk away, or price in heavily before you waive inspection. **Face** = cosmetic, good phased-cash value. The test: peeling paint from a roof leak, bubbling floor from a slow supply leak, and cracked tile from a rotted subfloor are bones wearing face clothing.
Bones — price in heavily

### Foundation & structure
Horizontal or stair-step block cracking, bowing/leaning basement walls, sloping floors you can feel, doors/windows sticking in several rooms, re-cracked patches, sagging ridge, rot at sill plates. Much stock is 1900–1940s — Clarksburg median year built among active listings reported as 1920 ([EZ Home Search](https://www.ezhomesearch.com/us/wv/clarksburg/houses/)) — with block, stone or brick foundations on hillside lots. Any bowing wall or active movement needs a structural engineer, not just a general inspection.

### Roof
Ask for install year/paperwork. Curling/missing shingles, multiple layers, sagging decking, active leaks, stained ceilings, rotted fascia. Full asphalt replacement is ~$5,900–$15,000+ nationally (see §2). An end-of-life roof also blocks insurance and many loans.

### Electrical — panel & wiring
**Federal Pacific (FPE / Stab-Lok) and Zinsco / GTE-Sylvania:** documented breaker failure-to-trip, overheating and melting to the bus bar; many insurers refuse, surcharge, or require replacement. **Knob-and-tube** (pre-1950s): no grounding, deteriorated insulation. **Aluminium branch wiring** (1960s–70s): loosens at connections, fire risk — may need approved pigtailing / COPALUM-type repair rather than automatic full rewire. Fuse boxes, 60-amp service, ungrounded outlets = panel + partial rewire budget. Modern baseline for a 3-bed with AC is 200-amp. Sources: [Bluefield Group](https://www.bluefieldgroup.com/blog/insurance-considerations-for-older-homes-with-outdated-wiring-or-plumbing/), [Vetcon](https://vetconelectricians.com/blog/the-buyers-guide-to-federal-pacific-zinsco-panels-in-ocala-homes/).

### Plumbing material
**Galvanised steel** (pre-1960s) corrodes inside — test by running bath + kitchen together. **Polybutylene** (1975–1995, grey/blue) has a history of premature failure; replacement is frequently required for insurance. **Cast iron drains** at 50+ years: budget a sewer camera scope — a failed lateral is a bones cost. Also check lead service line/solder, Orangeburg sewer, low pressure, slow drains in lowest fixtures, stains under traps, and whether the shut-off works. Copper and PEX are low-risk. Source: Bluefield Group (above).

### HVAC & water heater
Record make/model/serial-plate year. Furnace/AC at 15–20+ years, water heater at 10–15 years, rust, or no service records = price in replacement. Tank heaters typically last 10–15 years (Angi). No heat to upper floor, cracked heat exchanger, or unvented heaters are walk-away-until-quoted.
Face — good value

### Cosmetic wins (phased cash)
- Interior paint, patch/skim, trim/caulk, door hardware, lighting, outlet/switch covers (not wiring)
- Refinishing sound hardwood; LVP over sound subfloor
- Cabinet paint/reface + hardware, laminate / butcher-block counters, backsplash
- Stock vanity, mirror, fixtures where plumbing stays in place
- Reglazing/repairing sound windows, landscaping cleanup, pressure washing
- Front-door paint/replacement, mailbox, house numbers, gutter cleaning

### Water, drainage, basements & hillsides — the regional pattern
Full basements + lots sloping *toward* the house + downspouts discharging at the foundation + clay soils + old or no drain tile. Look for efflorescence (white salts), musty odour, tide lines, sump with no backup, mould on walls/joists, and fresh paint *only* on basement walls. Check retaining walls for lean/bulge and neighbours' runoff. Exterior grading + gutter extensions are cheap; interior waterproofing or excavation is not. Even outside a mapped flood zone, low lots near creeks/runs in the Clarksburg / Fairmont / Philippi valleys warrant a direct question and an insurance quote.

### Septic, sewer & well
Public sewer or septic? Age, last pumped, county health record? A failing drainfield is five figures and can block loans. The WV Housing Development Fund runs a [Residential Septic Loan Program](https://www.wvhdf.com/programs/single-family-programs-and-resources/the-residential-septic-loan-program/) for failing septic / sewer connection for owner-occupiers — a fallback, not a substitute for inspection. For wells: flow, treatment, water test.

### Mould, lead & asbestos
Mould is a water problem first — fix the source leak, don't paint over it. **Lead:** EPA notes pre-1978 homes are more likely to have lead paint (federal ban 1978); the Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) rule requires lead-safe certified firms for paid work in pre-1978 housing. RRP generally does not apply to a homeowner in their own owner-occupied home, but *does* apply if you rent it out or flip — directly relevant to the keep/rent/sell plan. Sources: [EPA — Reduce Lead Risk](https://www.epa.gov/lead/reduce-risk-lead-exposure-home), [EPA — DIY Lead-Safe](https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-safe-renovations-diyers). **Asbestos:** suspect pipe/boiler wrap, vinyl tile & mastic, siding, roofing, textured coatings. Test before disturbance; intact material is often managed in place. Confidence on any specific house without sampling: **low**.

### Coal-region specifics
Staged additions with mixed wiring/plumbing generations, coal chutes / converted coal furnaces, chimneys beyond lining life, unpermitted basement “bedrooms” without egress. Count legal beds/baths as what has egress, heat, and permits — not listing copy. Add radon, termites in damp sills, and buried oil tanks to inspection checks.
**⚠ West Virginia mine subsidence insurance — keep it, don't waive it.**
Coverage for loss caused by collapse of man-made underground coal mines (WV Code §33-30). It is sold as part of / an endorsement to a homeowners policy, reinsured through the state's Mine Subsidence Insurance Fund operated by BRIM — up to **$200,000 per structure**, over 15,000 policies a year. Deductible in statute: $250–$500.
Under §33-30-6, every policy insuring a structure in WV must *include* subsidence coverage at a separately stated premium *unless waived* — except in a listed set of mostly non-coal counties. **Harrison, Marion and Barbour are NOT in that exception list**, so the default here is include-unless-you-waive. New coverage takes effect on the 30th calendar day after application.
**Cost:** No current BRIM premium schedule was found (Oct 2026). ValuePenguin lists WV at ~$48/year in its state comparison ([ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/mine-subsidence-home-insurance)) — secondary, low confidence on the exact dollar, but the takeaway holds: tens of dollars a year, not hundreds. Get the exact premium on your own quote, ask whether the property is over/near mapped workings, and note subsidence is *not* flood, earthquake, landslide, or general settlement cover. Sources: [WV Code §33-30-6 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/west-virginia/2023/chapter-33/article-30/section-33-30-6/), [BRIM](https://brim.wv.gov/media/117/download?inline).

## 3 Renovation financing, plainly
**Common mechanics:** In 203(k), HomeStyle and VA renovation loans, repair money is *not* handed to you at closing. It sits in a lender-controlled escrow and is released in draws as work is completed and inspected, to a licensed contractor. DIY is generally not allowed (203(k), VA) or heavily restricted (HomeStyle). Work must start promptly (203(k): generally within 30 days) and finish inside a programme window. Closing is slower than a plain purchase.

### FHA 203(k) — Limited

Close ~45–60 days
**For:** Non-structural / cosmetic + systems work, owner-occupied 1–4 unit primary residence; house must be habitable. **Cap:** Up to $75,000 total rehab (raised from $35,000 by HUD Mortgagee Letter 2024-13, from 4 Nov 2024); no minimum; up to 9 months; up to 2 draws; no HUD consultant required. **Down:** 3.5% on purchase + rehab at 580+ credit (10% at 500–579 under standard FHA; most lenders' practical floor ~580–620). **County limit:** 2026 FHA 1-unit limit $541,287 for Harrison, Marion & Barbour — not binding here ([LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/home/fha/fha-loan-limits-in-west-virginia/)).
**Pros**
- Lowest down payment for financed rehab
- Covers roof, HVAC, kitchens, baths
**Cons**
- FHA mortgage insurance — upfront + monthly, often for life at minimum down
- Fewer willing lenders/contractors; slower close
Sources: [Delaware Mortgage — 203(k) guide (ML 2024-13)](https://delawaremortgageloans.net/fha-203k-rehab-loans/), [Priority Home](https://www.mypriorityhome.com/loan-options/fha-203k-loan/), timeline [lender guide (medium confidence)](https://jhenesismortgage.com/fha-203k-loan-florida-2026/).

### FHA 203(k) — Standard

Close ~60–90 days
**For:** Structural work, additions, foundation, major rehab, uninhabitable houses. Minimum $5,000 rehab; no separate rehab cap (limited by FHA county limit / after-improved value). HUD-approved consultant **required** (scope + draw inspections, up to 5 draws); up to 12 months; may finance up to 6 months of mortgage payments if unoccupiable. Max loan is the lesser of 96.5% of purchase + rehab or 110% of after-improved value.
**Pros**
- Only mainstream route that finances a true structural fixer in one loan
**Cons**
- Most process, consultant fees, longest close
- Contractor must tolerate draw inspections
Sources: Delaware guides above; [FHA Lenders](https://fhalenders.com/fha-203k-loan/).

### Fannie Mae HomeStyle Renovation (conventional)

Close ~45–60 days
**For:** Cosmetic *and* structural; 1–4 unit primary, plus 1-unit second homes and 1-unit investment properties — unlike FHA/VA owner-occupied-only. **Down/credit:** As low as 3% for a 1-unit primary (5% is the common framing), ~620 minimum credit, DTI typically ≤45%, no income limit. **Cap:** Rehab ≤75% of the lesser of (purchase + rehab) or as-completed value; must fit conforming limits (2026 baseline reported for Harrison Co. $832,750 — not binding). No minimum rehab; complete within 12 months. Funds in escrow; contractor draws after inspection; initial materials draw up to 50% in lender summaries.
**Pros**
- PMI falls off at 20% equity (unlike FHA MIP)
- Luxury items allowed; investor/second-home flexibility
**Cons**
- Harder credit bar than FHA; same draw friction
- Fewer local lenders offer it
Sources: [MoneyTips](https://moneytips.com/mortgages/types-of-mortgages/fannie-mae-homestyle-loan/), [Rocket Mortgage](https://www.rocketmortgage.com/learn/fannie-mae-homestyle-loan), [RenoFi](https://www.Renofi.com/renovation-loans/fannie-mae-homestyle-renovation-loans/).

### USDA — two different things, don't confuse them

Address-specific
**(a) USDA purchase (Section 502 Guaranteed / Direct)** — a purchase mortgage, *not* a renovation loan. Guaranteed: 0% down via an approved lender for eligible rural addresses, households ≤115% of area median income. The house must meet USDA property standards as-is — a true fixer needing major repairs often *fails* unless repairs are minor or done via escrow agreement; there is no built-in 203(k)-style rehab budget. Eligibility is by **exact address**, not county. Barbour/Philippi, Lost Creek, Stonewood and rural fringes are more likely eligible than central Bridgeport/Fairmont/Clarksburg — but check every address on the official USDA map. Confidence until an official address check: **low**. Direct: USDA lends directly to low/very-low-income buyers; slow, funding-dependent.
**(b) USDA Section 504 Repair Loans & Grants** — for people who *already own and occupy* the home. Current official terms: loan max $40,000, grant max $10,000, combined $50,000; loan 20 years at fixed 1%; grants $10,000 lifetime limit, repayable if sold in <3 years; grants only age 62+ and only to remove health/safety hazards. Must be unable to obtain affordable credit elsewhere and at/below county very-low/low limits. Older pages quoting $20,000 / $7,500 are **outdated**. Relevance: a post-purchase contingency for a qualifying owner facing a safety failure — not a way to finance the purchase. Source: [USDA Rural Development — Section 504](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/single-family-housing-programs/single-family-housing-repair-loans-grants?mf_ct_campaign=yahoo-synd-feed).

### VA renovation — only if VA-eligible

Close ~30–45 days where offered
**For:** Veterans / eligible service members / surviving spouses with a Certificate of Eligibility, primary residence only, meeting VA Minimum Property Requirements *after* renovation. Rides on a VA purchase or cash-out refi (not IRRRL); 0% down possible, funding fee applies (waived for certain disability ratings), no monthly mortgage insurance. Caps vary by lender because VA sets the framework and lenders set overlays: CrossCountry caps renovation at $50,000 / 60 days, non-structural; others advertise up to $100,000 / ~120 days. No DIY; fixed-price, lender-accepted licensed contractor (VA rescinded the Builder ID requirement 31 Mar 2025, Circular 26-25-01).
**Pros**
- Best economics if eligible: 0 down, no PMI
**Cons**
- Hardest to find — many lenders don't offer it
- Habitability/safety scope only; MPR appraisal can kill a rough house
Sources: [Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/blog/what-is-a-va-renovation-loan/), [CrossCountry](https://crosscountrymortgage.com/mortgage/loans/renovation/va-renovation/), [Security America](https://securityamericamortgage.com/va-renovation-loan/).

### Phased cash — pay as you go (the base plan)

Plain loan: 30–45 days
Buy with a *plain* mortgage — which requires the house to already pass that loan's property standards — then work monthly: Year 0–1 safety + water + heat, Year 1–2 kitchen/bath refresh, Year 2–3 floors/paint/exterior.
**Pros**
- No consultant, draws, or contractor-paperwork premium; DIY allowed for cosmetics (subject to lead RRP if you later rent/flip)
- Work can stop any month; no interest on work not yet done — at ~7.2–7.3%, financing $30k of cosmetics for 30 years costs far more than the work
**Cons**
- You need cash *and* an emergency reserve at once; a month-3 bones failure eats the cosmetic budget
- Some “cosmetic” houses fail a plain appraisal — peeling paint (FHA/VA/USDA), missing flooring, non-functioning heat, end-of-life roof are lender-required repairs. Get that list in writing
**Hybrid that often fits best here:** plain loan + seller credits / seller-completed lender-required repairs + phased cash for everything the lender does *not* require, with Section 504 / WVHDF septic help as contingency for qualifying safety items only.

## 5 Five biggest mistakes — and how to avoid each

### 1. Waiving or shrinking inspection to “win” the house
Keep a full inspection contingency, plus specialists triggered by age: sewer camera, septic inspection, electrician (FPE/Zinsco/knob-and-tube), roofer, structural engineer if walls/floors move. In a 1920s Clarksburg/Fairmont house the general inspection is triage, not diagnosis. If a seller won't allow specialists, price as if the worst case is true — or walk.

### 2. Budgeting for the face, discovering the bones after closing
Sequence bones-first (water out, roof, panel, heat, sewer/septic) and hold a **20–30% contingency** on top of quotes — not a generic 10–20%. Don't install LVP, cabinets or paint in a room that still leaks, has polybutylene above it, or needs a rewire inside its walls. Every cosmetic dollar spent before that room's bones are cleared risks being torn out.

### 3. Over-improving for the street — especially a major kitchen/bath
Cap total basis (purchase + all rehab) at the price of the best renovated comparable sales within ~1 mile / same town, not at what you spent. In Bridgeport that ceiling is high; in Clarksburg/Philippi it arrives fast. Choose the CvV minor scope. Keep receipts and before/after photos for the appraisal/refinance file.

### 4. Assuming a renovation loan, USDA, or phased cash will work — unchecked
Before offer, ask a *local* lender who actually closes 203(k)/HomeStyle in Harrison/Marion: Will this house, in this condition, pass a plain appraisal? Exactly which repairs will be required? Which licensed contractors/consultants here will sign a draw contract? In rural Barbour Co. that contractor may not exist at your price. Check USDA eligibility by exact address, not county.

### 5. Ignoring insurance, subsidence, lead/asbestos & exit rules until after purchase
Get a real homeowners quote *during* inspection (panel, roof age, plumbing, knob-and-tube can make a house uninsurable or surcharged), keep mine subsidence cover, test for lead/asbestos *before* demo, and remember RRP lead-safe rules bite when you later rent or flip a pre-1978 house — and a Section 504 grant is repayable on sale within 3 years. Budget carrying costs at ~7.2–7.3% for every month a “3-month” plan slips. Phased plans slip.

## 6 Viewing checklist — in person

### Before you go
- Print listing, tax record (year built, sq ft, lot), seller's disclosure if available
- Bring phone (photos/video + flash), tape, marble/ball for floor slope, outlet tester if you own one, notebook, mask for basement/attic dust

### Outside — photo all four sides, roof, slope, drainage
- Roof: age signs, missing/curling shingles, sag, chimney flashing/cap, gutters — where does water discharge?
- Siding/trim: rot, peeling (lead-era?), asbestos-look siding, stone/brick cracks
- Foundation above grade: cracks, patching, bowing, additions' joints
- Lot: slope toward house? Retaining walls leaning? Buried tank pipes, septic cleanout, well head
- Porch/deck structure, steps, railings

### Inside, room by room
- Floors: slope, bounce, rot at baths/kitchen, hardwood under carpet?
- Walls/ceilings: crack shape/location, stains, fresh patch in one spot only
- Windows/doors: open/close, rot, fogged panes, egress in every bedroom
- Kitchen/bath: run water, pressure with two fixtures on, flush, look under every sink, soft floor around toilet/tub, fans vent where?
- Smell, room by room: musty, sewer, oil/gas, animal, smoke

### Basement / crawl / attic — photograph the data plates
- Foundation walls: bowing, horizontal cracks, water lines, efflorescence, sump (backup?), posts/jacks — temporary or permanent?
- Panel: photo the label — brand (FPE/Zinsco?), amps, fuse vs breaker, rust
- Wiring visible: knob-and-tube, aluminium, cloth, DIY splices
- Plumbing visible: material/colour, shut-off works?, sewer cleanout, lead-look service line
- HVAC + water heater: plate year, fuel, service stickers, rust/leaks, flue, ducts, returns upstairs?
- Joists/sills: rot, termite tubes, mould, insulation, K&T under attic insulation; coal chute / old furnace remains

### Ask seller/agent — in writing where it matters
- Roof, furnace/AC, water heater ages + paperwork/service? Known leaks, flooding, basement water, sewer backups?
- Sewer or septic? Septic age, last pumped, permits? Water: public or well, any tests?
- Electrical/plumbing updates: what, when, permitted, by whom?
- Insurance claims? Mine subsidence cover/claims, known mines, foundation repairs with transferable warranty?
- Unpermitted additions / basement bedrooms? Lead/asbestos known? Oil tank present/removed with paperwork?
- What stays, utility bills by season, internet provider/speed (critical in rural Barbour/Lost Creek), rental history / restrictions, flood insurance ever required?

### Same day, after viewing
- File photos by house; write bones list vs face list; total a rough bones quote list
- Decide: offer as-is, offer with inspection + specialist access, or walk — don't let one pretty kitchen outvote a bowed basement wall
