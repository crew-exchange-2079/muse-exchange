## 2. Inspection add-ons — price support and statement truth

**2.1 Radon testing ($150–$250).** Price is the low end only: Angi 2026 puts professional testing at $145–$714, average $421 — soften to "from ~$150; typical ~$400" or cite the DIY-kit alternative. "Essential" is supported — EPA: all homes should be tested; fix at 4 pCi/L or higher, consider fixing at 2–4. "Elevated risks due to rocky soil" is **misstated**: EPA attributes radon to *uranium content* in rock and soil (radium-226/radon-222 are present in almost all rock and soil), and much of Appalachia is EPA Zone 1/2 — but EPA also warns the zone map "should not be used to determine if a home in a given zone should be tested." "Fall/winter is the best time" is **not EPA's wording**: EPA requires closed-house conditions for short-term tests, satisfiable in any season; the winter preference comes from state guidance (e.g. PA DEP: winter gives more accurate results because windows stay closed). Defensible if reworded and not attributed to EPA.

**2.2 Well water testing ($100–$300).** Price sits inside published ranges (Angi: lab testing roughly $50–$500 by panel). The analyte list needs a frequency fix: EPA's *annual* list is coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids and pH. Lead and heavy metals are first-use/plumbing-specific tests (EPA ties lead testing to homes whose plumbing contains lead), not an annual blanket. "Standard inspectors do not evaluate water quality" is **true** — InterNACHI's Standards of Practice expressly exclude determining water quality or potability.

**2.3 Septic inspection ($300–$600).** Price supported (Angi: average $550, normal range $200–$900). Substance supported: EPA SepticSmart says systems should be inspected at least every three years *by a septic service professional* and tanks typically pumped every three to five years. One overstatement: the guide bundles "pump the tank and inspect" as one step, but EPA prescribes pumping *when measured scum/sludge levels require it*, not automatically at every inspection. (Baffle inspection is standard practice; EPA's text covers scum/sludge layers and the outlet rather than a baffle checklist.)

**2.4 Pest / WDI inspection ($75–$150).** Price supported (Angi: WDO average $150; Thumbtack: termite inspection $85–$150 typical). Soil-to-wood contact as a risk factor is confirmed by NPMA's own consumer advisory (earth-to-wood contact heads its list). But no primary source found states WDI is especially "crucial for older homes" or singles out wood siding — those are the guide's editorializing; soften or drop the wood-siding phrase.

**2.5 Chimney Level 2 inspection ($200–$400).** Price within Angi's published Level 2 ranges ($100–$500 / $200–$1,000 by page); CSIA publishes no prices. **The statement is not true as written:** NFPA 211 does not make Level 2 "essential" merely because a home has a fireplace or woodstove. Level 2 is required *upon sale or transfer of a property*, after a malfunction or external event that may have damaged the chimney, and when the system changes (relining, new appliance/fuel). The annual inspection for routine use is Level 1. Because this guide is for buyers, a transfer *is* happening — so Level 2 is the right recommendation, but the guide must give the transfer as the reason, not the fireplace. "Many older chimneys lack proper liners" is plausible but unquantified in any primary source checked — soften to "older chimneys may lack liners."

**2.6 Structural engineer ($400–$800).** Price supported (Angi: structural report ~$550 average, range $350–$800). The trigger list (foundation cracks, bowing walls, sagging floors) matches Angi's own warning signs almost exactly. Add a caveat: a foundation-focused evaluation can run to $1,200+, above the guide's high.

## 3. Checklist and calendar — flags

- **Scorecard, "Downspouts extended 5ft+?"** — supported: getting discharge several feet from the foundation is standard guidance (commonly 4–6 ft), and 5 ft+ is a fair pass mark. No change needed.
- **Scorecard, HVAC "(>15 yrs?)"** — reasonable as a *budget-soon* flag, but age alone isn't a fail: furnaces commonly last 15–20 years and heat pumps 10–15, so ">15" over-flags furnaces and under-flags heat pumps. Soften to "(age? heat pump >10–15 yrs / furnace >15–20 yrs = budget for replacement)."
- **Calendar, October "retest for radon"** — **wrong cadence.** EPA's Citizen's Guide: retest every two years, and after major renovations or a mitigation install. An annual October retest isn't EPA guidance. Replace with a biennial heating-season test (see action list).
- **Calendar, December "Close crawlspace vents to prevent freezing"** — **misleading as a blanket rule.** DOE Building America guidance for closed crawlspaces is that all foundation vents be sealed *permanently*; for vented crawlspaces, seasonal closing is traditional but can trap moisture, and frozen pipes are better prevented by insulating/sealing and heat tape on exposed pipes. Soften to a conditional item.
- **Calendar, January "check attic for ice dams"** — fine as written (checking is harmless); worth one clause: the durable fix is attic air-sealing, insulation and ventilation, not ice removal.
- **Calendar, May "Test well water"** — supported: annual testing matches EPA (coliform/nitrates), and spring is a sensible slot.
- **Calendar, May "pump septic tank if due"** — supported as written ("if due" matches EPA's 3–5-year, levels-based pumping).
- **Scorecard, foundation cracks (horizontal/stepping)** — supported: horizontal cracks are the serious structural warning (consistent with the engineer triggers in §2.6). No change.

## How I checked it

Every cost row was checked on at least two opened cost-guide pages (Angi, HomeGuide, Fixr — 2026 data/editions), and every safety statement against the primary source named for it: EPA radon pages and the EPA Home Buyer's and Seller's Guide, EPA private-wells guidance, EPA SepticSmart materials, NFPA 211 inspection levels as published by CSIA/chimney professionals, NPMA's own consumer advisory, and InterNACHI's Standards of Practice. Calendar items were checked against EPA (radon cadence, well/septic intervals) and DOE Building America (crawlspace sealing). Quotes are short verbatim excerpts; everything else is paraphrase. The guide's own text was read in full from the task folder.

## Unsure about

- County-by-county EPA radon zone assignments for specific Appalachian counties could not be rendered from EPA's map page during checking; the Zone 1/2 characterisation of "much of Appalachia" is EPA-map-level, not county-verified. It doesn't change the recommendation (EPA says test every home regardless of zone).
- No cost source publishes a WV/Appalachian regional adjustment, so the cheat sheet remains national data; rural WV quotes will vary in both directions.
- CSIA publishes no inspection prices, and no current csia.org page spelling out Level 2 triggers in CSIA's own words could be opened; the trigger list comes from NFPA 211 as summarised unanimously by chimney-industry sources.
- "Many older chimneys lack proper liners" and WDI being "crucial for older homes" could not be confirmed or refuted in a primary source — both are flagged as unquantified editorial claims, not as false.
- Fixr's roof page showed no visible update date; its figures are labelled 2026 by the page's edition, which is the weakest dating among the cost sources used.
