# Best US bank account opening bonuses for a West Virginia resident — checked 2026-11-21

Neutral comparison only. No account is recommended for any named person. All figures were read from the bank's own offer page first, then cross-checked against bonus trackers (NerdWallet, The Penny Hoarder, Doctor of Credit, BankBonus.com) on 2026-11-21. Offers change or end without notice; confirm the live terms on the bank's own page before opening anything. Bonuses paid on deposit accounts are generally taxable as interest (reported on IRS Form 1099-INT) — Chase states this on its offer page.

Scope: personal checking and savings bonuses only. Business accounts are excluded. Any offer needing more than $5,000 parked in the account was excluded (see "Excluded by the $5,000-parked rule" below). Direct-deposit totals are not "parked" money — the paycheck flows through — so offers that need large direct-deposit totals are included and flagged where the total is high.

## Which can run at the same time off one split paycheck

Yes — most of these can be stacked, because each one only needs a slice of the same paycheck sent to a different account. Most employers' payroll systems let a paycheck be split across several accounts by dollar amount or percentage, and each bank counts its own slice as a qualifying direct deposit as long as it arrives as payroll ACH. The practical stack, in the order the deposits should be pointed, is:

1. **Chase ($300)** — any direct-deposit amount qualifies, so even a small slice works.
2. **Huntington ($400)** — $500 total.
3. **Fifth Third ($300)** — $500 total.
4. **Capital One ($250)** — 2 deposits of $500 each ($1,000 total).
5. **Wells Fargo ($325)** — $1,000 total.
6. **SoFi ($150 tier)** — $1,000 total; or $5,000 total for the $500 tier.

That core stack needs about $4,000–$4,500 of direct deposits spread across the accounts within 75–90 days (roughly two to three pay periods for a typical paycheck), for $1,725 in bonuses at the lower SoFi tier. Adding Truist ($400, needs $2,000 + 20 debit purchases), PNC ($100 for $500, or $400 for $5,000), Citadel ($300, needs $2,000), SECU ($350, needs two payroll deposits), and Bank of America ($300 tier, needs $5,000) is possible only if the paycheck is large enough to cover the extra deposit totals within each offer's window — the deposit totals are the binding constraint, not the number of accounts.

Cautions on stacking:

- **Don't double-count the same dollars.** A dollar of paycheck sent to Chase cannot also count toward Huntington. The totals above are additive.
- **Each bank's direct-deposit definition differs.** Payroll ACH counts everywhere. Some banks also accept ACH pushes from another bank as a qualifying direct deposit (reported for Capital One and U.S. Bank), but others do not, and the banks' own pages generally define a qualifying deposit as employer payroll, pension, or government benefits. Do not assume an internal transfer counts — see "Unsure about".
- **Debit-purchase offers add real work.** Truist's 20 purchases and Capital One's 20-purchase alternative route need the card to actually be used; the direct-deposit routes are less work.
- **Watch the clocks.** The windows differ: Capital One 75 days, PNC 60 days, SoFi 31 days for the deposit, Chase/Wells Fargo/Fifth Third/Huntington/BofA/Citadel/SECU 90 days, Truist 120 days. SoFi's 31-day window is the tightest.
- **Fees while waiting.** Chase ($15), Wells Fargo ($15), Bank of America ($12), Huntington ($10), and PNC (unsure) charge monthly fees unless the waiver is met — the direct deposit itself usually waives the fee at Chase, Wells Fargo, BofA, and Huntington, but check each account's waiver before letting a balance sit.
- **Eligibility is per bank, not per person across banks.** Opening a Chase account does not disqualify anyone from Huntington, Fifth Third, Capital One, or any other bank's bonus. Each bank only excludes people who recently had an account or a bonus with that same bank.

## Chase-recent-bonus exclusion

- **Chase's own rule:** A recent Chase checking bonus blocks a new Chase checking bonus — one new checking bonus and one new savings bonus only every two years from the last offer enrollment date. A Chase account closed within the last 90 days, or closed with a negative balance within the last 3 years, also blocks eligibility. This is stated on Chase's own offer page.
- **Other banks:** No other bank in this comparison was found to exclude people because they recently received a Chase bonus. The exclusions found are all about the same bank: Wells Fargo (no checking bonus in the prior 12 months), Bank of America (no BofA personal checking in the last 12 months), PNC (no PNC checking bonus in the past 24 months), Fifth Third (no Fifth Third deposit account in the last 13 months), Citadel (no Citadel checking bonus before, lifetime limit), Truist (no Truist checking closed on or after 2025-07-14), Capital One (no 360/Simply/Total Control checking since 2024-01-01 per NerdWallet). A recent Chase bonus is not, by itself, a disqualifier at any of them.
